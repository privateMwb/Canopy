Using a CDN

A CDN puts copies of your content on servers near your users. It cuts latency, takes load off your origin, and is one of the simplest big wins available.

§1What a CDN Does

A content delivery network is a global network of servers called edge locations or points of presence. When a user requests a file, they're routed to a nearby edge. If the edge has a copy, it answers directly. If not, it fetches from your origin, stores a copy, and answers.

BenefitHow
Lower latencyShorter distance means fewer milliseconds per round trip
Faster connectionsEdges terminate TCP and TLS close to the user
Less origin loadMost requests never reach your servers
ResilienceEdges can absorb spikes and some outages
Modern protocolsMost CDNs offer HTTP/2, HTTP/3, and Brotli out of the box

§2Setting One Up

  1. Choose a provider that fits your needs and budget. Many offer generous free tiers.
  2. Point the CDN at your origin, so it knows where to fetch from.
  3. Route traffic through it, either by serving assets from a CDN hostname such as cdn.example.com, or by putting the CDN in front of your whole domain with DNS.
  4. Set caching headers on the origin. The CDN obeys them, and s-maxage gives you CDN-specific control.
  5. Verify that responses are coming from the edge and are hits.
# Example response headers when a CDN served from cache
HTTP/2 200
cache-control: public, max-age=31536000, immutable
age: 4812
x-cache: HIT

Header names for hit status differ between providers. Check yours for the exact field.

§3What to Cache, and Watch-Outs

  • Static assets (JS, CSS, images, fonts) are the easy win. Cache them aggressively with fingerprinted URLs.
  • HTML can be cached at the edge with short lifetimes or revalidation. This is powerful for mostly static sites.
  • Personalized content must not be shared. Use private, or vary correctly, or don't cache it.
  • Cookies can make a CDN treat each request as unique and bypass its cache. Strip them from static asset requests.
  • Dynamic APIs can often be cached briefly, which smooths load even with a short lifetime.

Also think about cost and control. Egress and request fees vary, and a CDN adds another system to configure. Misconfigured caching is one of the more common causes of 'the site shows old content' tickets.

The key insight A CDN is a cache plus a network. Most of its benefit appears only if your responses are cacheable, so get the headers right first.

§4Common Mistakes

MistakeWhat HappensFix
Putting a CDN in front without caching headersEverything misses and goes to originSet Cache-Control so the edge can store responses
Caching a personalized page publiclyOne user's data is shown to othersMark it private, or exclude it from the cache
Sending cookies with static asset requestsThe CDN may bypass its cacheServe static assets from a cookieless path or domain
Practice
  1. Put a free-tier CDN in front of a small static site. Compare the load time of one asset from two distant locations before and after.
  2. Explain in two sentences why a CDN can still be slow if a response has Cache-Control: no-store.
Key takeaways
  • A CDN serves cached copies from edge locations near users and reduces both latency and origin load.
  • It obeys your caching headers, so getting them right matters more than the provider.
  • Keep personalized responses out of shared caches, and watch for cookies defeating caching.
