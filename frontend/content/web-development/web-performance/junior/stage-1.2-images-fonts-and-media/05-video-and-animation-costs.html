Video & Animation Costs

Video and animation can dominate a page's weight and a device's battery. Know what each costs, and pick the cheapest tool that does the job.

§1Video: Weight and Loading

A single autoplaying video can outweigh the rest of the page combined. Treat video as the heaviest asset type and be deliberate about how it loads.

  • Don't autoplay with sound. It's blocked by browsers and annoying anyway.
  • Don't preload everything. Use preload="none" or "metadata" for video the user may never play.
  • Add a poster image so there is something to show immediately, and lazy load the player below the fold.
  • Encode efficiently. Modern codecs, such as H.265, VP9, and AV1, produce much smaller files at the same quality than older ones, but support varies, so offer fallbacks.
  • Use adaptive streaming (HLS or DASH) for long video, so each viewer gets a bitrate that suits their connection.
<video controls preload="none" poster="intro.jpg" width="960" height="540">
  <source src="intro.webm" type="video/webm">
  <source src="intro.mp4" type="video/mp4">
</video>

§2Animated GIFs Are a Trap

GIF is a 1980s format. It has no inter-frame compression to speak of, so a short animation can weigh megabytes. A muted looping video is typically a tiny fraction of the size and decodes more efficiently.

<!-- Replaces an animated GIF: small, efficient, behaves the same -->
<video autoplay loop muted playsinline width="480" height="270">
  <source src="demo.webm" type="video/webm">
  <source src="demo.mp4" type="video/mp4">
</video>

For simple UI motion, such as a spinner, a fade, or an illustration, prefer CSS animation or an SVG over a bitmap or video, because they are resolution-independent and tiny.

§3Animation Costs Beyond Bytes

Animation also costs CPU, GPU and battery. Animating properties that force layout, like top, width, or margin, makes the browser recompute positions on every frame. Animating transform and opacity can be handled by the compositor and is far cheaper. A later lesson goes deeper.

NeedCheapest tool
Looping product demoMuted <video>
Spinner, fade, hover effectCSS transitions on transform and opacity
Logo or illustration motionSVG with CSS
Long-form videoAdaptive streaming with a poster

Respect users who ask for less motion. Wrap non-essential animation in a prefers-reduced-motion check, which is also kinder to low-power devices.

Gotcha Hero video backgrounds look great in a demo and wreck LCP on a phone. If you use one, serve a poster, keep it short and small, and skip it on slow connections.

§4Common Mistakes

MistakeWhat HappensFix
Using an animated GIF for a demo clipMegabytes for a few seconds of motionUse a muted looping video
Letting video preload by defaultBandwidth wasted on videos never playedUse preload="none" or "metadata", plus a poster
Animating width or topLayout on every frame and jankAnimate transform and opacity
Practice
  1. Convert one animated GIF to MP4 and WebM with ffmpeg, and compare the sizes.
  2. Replace a layout-animating hover effect with a transform-based one, and compare the frame rate in DevTools.
Key takeaways
  • Video is the heaviest asset: lazy load it, add a poster, avoid default preload, and encode efficiently.
  • Replace GIFs with muted looping video, and use CSS or SVG for simple motion.
  • Animate transform and opacity, and honor prefers-reduced-motion.
