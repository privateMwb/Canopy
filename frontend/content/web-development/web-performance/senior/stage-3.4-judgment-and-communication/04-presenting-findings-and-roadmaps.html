Presenting Findings & Roadmaps

A correct analysis that nobody acts on is worth nothing. Presenting findings is about turning evidence into a decision for an audience with limited time and different concerns.

§1Know Your Audience

Different people need different things from the same data. Decide who you're talking to before you decide what to say.

AudienceCares aboutGive them
ExecutivesOutcomes, cost, risk, decisions neededA one-page summary: impact, ask, and timeline
Product managersTrade-offs with features, user impactPrioritized options with effort and expected effect
EngineersCause, approach, how to verifyTraces, specifics, and clear acceptance criteria
Design and marketingExperience and constraintsConcrete changes and what they preserve

Lead with what you want them to know or decide, then the evidence. Most audiences will not read past the first screen.

§2Structuring the Story

  1. The headline: one sentence with the finding and its stakes. 'Our mobile checkout is slow enough to cost an estimated 2% to 4% of conversions.'
  2. The evidence: two or three charts that carry the argument, such as field metrics versus thresholds and conversion by speed bucket. Each should make one point, with a clear title that states the takeaway.
  3. The cause: what is driving it, shown briefly with a trace or waterfall. Keep the detail in an appendix.
  4. The options: two or three approaches, each with effort, expected effect, and risk, plus your recommendation.
  5. The ask: the decision, the resources, and the timeline.
  6. How we'll know: the metrics and checkpoints that will show it worked.
# One-page summary skeleton
Headline:      Mobile LCP is 4.1 s at p75 (threshold 2.5 s); ~38% of visits are 'poor'
Why it matters: conversion falls ~9% from fast to slow visits (A/B-validated range 3-7%)
Cause:         hero image found late; 410 KB blocking JS; slow TTFB from uncached HTML
Plan:          Phase 1 (2 wks) image + caching -> est. LCP 3.0 s | Phase 2 (6 wks) JS split -> 2.4 s
Ask:           2 engineers for 8 weeks; success = p75 LCP <= 2.5 s, no conversion drop

§3Roadmaps and Handling Pushback

  • Phase the roadmap. Short-term wins build trust, and later phases fund structural work. Show expected results at each stage.
  • Tie milestones to measurable metrics, not to activities. 'p75 LCP under 3 s' rather than 'optimize images'.
  • State assumptions and risks, and what would change the plan.
  • Show what you're not doing, and why, to prevent hidden scope creep.
  • Anticipate objections. 'Is it worth the cost?' needs your value estimate. 'Won't this delay features?' needs your sequencing. 'Is the data reliable?' needs your method.
  • Be candid about uncertainty. Ranges, confidence levels, and what you'd test first build more trust than false certainty.
  • Follow up. Report results against the plan, including misses, so the next ask is easier.

Skeptical audiences respond to evidence and honesty, not volume. Concede valid points, show your data, and focus on the decision. Credibility accumulates across presentations, and one overclaimed number can cost all of it.

The key insight Executives decide on outcomes, cost, and risk. Put the decision and its stakes first, keep the technical detail on call, and show exactly how success will be measured.

§4Common Mistakes

MistakeWhat HappensFix
Opening with methodology and metrics jargonThe audience tunes out before the pointLead with the finding, the stakes, and the ask
Roadmaps built from activitiesNo way to tell if it workedTie milestones to measurable outcomes
Overclaiming certaintyOne miss destroys credibilityPresent ranges, assumptions, and how you'll verify
Practice
  1. Write a one-page summary for a performance proposal following the skeleton, aimed at an executive.
  2. List the three toughest objections you expect and write a short, evidence-based answer to each.
Key takeaways
  • Tailor the message to the audience and lead with the finding, stakes, and decision.
  • Tell the story: headline, evidence, cause, options, ask, and how success will be measured.
  • Phase the roadmap, tie milestones to metrics, and earn trust with honest uncertainty and follow-through.
