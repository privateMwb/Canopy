Mobile & Low-End Device Performance

The median user is not on your hardware. Designing for slow CPUs, little memory, and weak networks is where most real performance gains and losses live.

§1Know Your Audience's Devices

A high-end phone may run JavaScript several times faster than a typical mid-range or budget phone, and some of your users are on devices slower still. The gap is mostly in the CPU and memory, not the network, and it grows with every kilobyte of JavaScript you ship.

ConstraintEffect
Slow CPUParsing, compiling, and running JS take much longer; long tasks are longer
Small memoryFewer tabs and less cache; heavy pages are killed or reloaded
Throttling when hot or low on batteryPerformance varies over a session
Slow or lossy networkRound trips and large payloads hurt more
Small, sometimes low-resolution screensLarge images are wasted, but touch targets still need space

Don't guess. Look at the device class distribution in your field data, using a segmentation such as device memory, CPU cores, or a user-agent-derived tier. Set your design targets from your slow quartile, not your median.

§2Designing for the Slow End

  • Set a JavaScript budget based on a representative low-end device, and treat it as a hard limit.
  • Do less on the client. Render on the server or at build time, and hydrate selectively.
  • Minimize main-thread work: avoid large synchronous tasks, and move work to workers where it pays off.
  • Be careful with animation and effects. Costly blur, shadows, and large layers hit weak GPUs hard.
  • Reduce data: smaller images, lighter fonts, and fewer requests.
  • Keep the DOM small and avoid deep nesting.
  • Be gentle with memory. Don't keep huge datasets or images in memory.
// Adapt to the device when it helps (progressive enhancement, not blocking)
const weak = (navigator.deviceMemory ?? 4) <= 2 ||
             (navigator.hardwareConcurrency ?? 4) <= 2;
const saveData = navigator.connection?.saveData === true;

if (weak || saveData) {
  disableHeavyEffects();
  loadLighterImages();
}

These signals are approximate and not available in every browser, so use them only to reduce work, never to gate essential functionality. A fast core experience that everyone gets is better than a branching one.

§3Testing on Real Constraints

Emulation helps but doesn't replace real hardware, because real devices throttle, share memory, and have different JIT behavior.

  1. Keep a cheap physical Android phone as a test device and use it regularly.
  2. Use DevTools CPU throttling (4× or 6×) and Slow 4G or 3G profiles as a quick approximation.
  3. Use WebPageTest or a device farm with a real low-end device and realistic network.
  4. Test cold, with a cleared cache, and on a battery-saver setting.
  5. Compare the field data for your slow segment against the lab results, and calibrate.

Make slow-device results visible to the team, for example as a CI job that runs against a throttled profile, so regressions in the slow experience are noticed, not just those in the average one.

The key insight Performance for the typical device is set by what the slowest meaningful group experiences. Design budgets for that group, and the rest benefit automatically.

§4Common Mistakes

MistakeWhat HappensFix
Developing and testing only on a flagship phoneProblems on typical devices stay invisibleTest on a low-end device and with throttling
Using device signals to block featuresWrong guesses lock users outUse them only to reduce optional work
Setting budgets from the medianThe slow tail still suffersTarget the slow quartile
Practice
  1. Segment your field data by device class and report p75 INP and LCP for the slowest segment compared with the fastest.
  2. Run a page under 6× CPU throttling and Slow 4G, list the three worst problems, and plan fixes.
Key takeaways
  • CPU and memory, not only network, separate slow devices from fast ones.
  • Budget for the low end, do less on the client, and use device signals only to reduce optional work.
  • Test on real low-end hardware and make slow-device results visible to the team.
