Text & Headings

Headings, paragraphs, lists, emphasis, and quotes carry the meaning of your text, and a clean heading outline is what lets people skim and navigate.

§1Headings Make an Outline

<h1> through <h6> mark levels of importance, not sizes. Use one <h1> that names the page, then <h2> for main sections and <h3> for subsections inside them. Never skip a level just to get a smaller font; that is CSS's job.

<h1>Baking Sourdough</h1>
  <h2>Ingredients</h2>
  <h2>Method</h2>
    <h3>Mixing</h3>
    <h3>Proofing</h3>
  <h2>Troubleshooting</h2>

Screen reader users often jump from heading to heading to scan a page, so the outline works like a table of contents.

§2Paragraphs and Lists

Wrap each paragraph in <p>. Extra whitespace and line breaks in your source are collapsed into single spaces, so spacing comes from CSS, not from blank lines or repeated <br>.

<ul>                      <!-- unordered: order doesn't matter -->
  <li>Flour</li>
  <li>Water</li>
</ul>

<ol>                      <!-- ordered: sequence matters -->
  <li>Mix</li>
  <li>Rest</li>
</ol>

<dl>                      <!-- term and description pairs -->
  <dt>Autolyse</dt>
  <dd>Resting flour and water before adding salt.</dd>
</dl>

§3Emphasis, Importance, and Quotes

Choose inline tags for meaning, not for looks. <em> means stress emphasis (changes how a sentence is read), <strong> means strong importance, and <blockquote> and <q> mark quoted text.

<p>You <em>must</em> preheat the oven. <strong>Warning:</strong> the tray is hot.</p>

<blockquote cite="https://example.com/source">
  <p>Bread is the simplest of foods and the hardest to master.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>She called it <q>a lifelong practice</q>.</p>

<b> and <i> still exist for text that is styled without added importance, such as a product name or a foreign phrase, but reach for <em> and <strong> first.

Outline test Delete everything except the headings. If what remains does not read like a sensible table of contents, your heading structure needs work.

§4Common Mistakes

MistakeWhat HappensFix
Choosing <h3> because it "looks right"The outline skips a level and confuses assistive technologyUse the correct level and adjust size with CSS
Using <br><br> to separate paragraphsMeaningless markup; paragraphs are not exposed as paragraphsUse separate <p> elements and style the gap with CSS
Using <blockquote> just to indent textEverything indented is announced as a quotationUse CSS margin or padding for indentation
Practice
  1. Write a recipe page with one h1, three h2 sections, an ordered method, and an unordered ingredient list.
  2. Take a paragraph from any article and mark one phrase with em and one with strong, explaining why each fits.
Key takeaways
  • Heading levels describe structure; choose them for the outline, not for size.
  • Use lists for lists and p for paragraphs; whitespace in source is collapsed.
  • Pick inline tags for what the text means: em for stress, strong for importance.
