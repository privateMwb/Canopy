Monitoring & Capacity Planning

A handful of metrics reveal most MongoDB problems early, and tracking their trends lets you add capacity before users feel the shortage.

§2Alerts

Alert on conditions that need a human, with thresholds set from your baseline.

  • Replication lag above a few seconds, or oplog window below your target.
  • Disk above about 70-80% used, since compaction and growth need room.
  • Connections approaching the limit.
  • Sustained cache pressure, or a rise in slow queries.
  • A member in a bad state, or no primary.

§3Capacity Planning

Capacity planning means projecting when a resource runs out. Look at the trend, not the current value.

  1. Measure growth in data size, document count, and operations per second over several months.
  2. Project when disk, RAM (working set), or throughput reach about 70% of the limit.
  3. Plan the response ahead of time: larger instances, more disk, archiving old data with TTL or an archive collection, or adding shards.
  4. Load-test before big events, using a copy of production data where possible.
The key insight Disk fills slowly and then suddenly, and the working set outgrows RAM gradually and then falls off a cliff. Trends give you weeks of warning, and spot checks give you minutes.

§4Common Mistakes

MistakeWhat HappensFix
Watching averages onlySpikes and slow tails are hiddenTrack percentiles and maximums
No alert until the disk is fullWrites fail, and recovery is painfulAlert at 70-80% and plan ahead
Sizing for today's dataSurprise outages when growth continuesProject from trends
Practice
  1. Choose eight metrics for a dashboard and write the alert threshold and the action for each.
  2. Given 40 GB growth per month and 500 GB of usable disk with 300 GB used, compute when to act.
Key takeaways
  • Watch throughput, latency, queries examined, cache, disk, connections, and replication lag.
  • Alert on conditions needing action, with thresholds based on your baseline.
  • Plan capacity from growth trends, well before limits are reached.
