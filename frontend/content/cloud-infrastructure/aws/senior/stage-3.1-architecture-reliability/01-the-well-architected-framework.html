The Well-Architected Framework

The Well-Architected Framework is a structured set of questions for reviewing a workload, and it is useful only when you actually run the review.

§1The Six Pillars

PillarCore question
Operational excellenceCan we deploy, observe, and improve safely?
SecurityAre identities, data, and infrastructure protected?
ReliabilityDoes it recover from failure and meet demand?
Performance efficiencyAre we using the right resources as needs change?
Cost optimizationAre we paying only for the value we get?
SustainabilityAre we minimizing environmental impact of the workload?

§2How to Use It

The framework gives each pillar design principles, best practices, and questions. The AWS Well-Architected Tool in the console walks a workload through those questions, records answers, flags high-risk issues, and tracks improvements over time.

  • Review a specific workload, not "the company".
  • Include the people who run it, not just the architects.
  • Record decisions and the risks you accept on purpose.
  • Prioritize by business impact; do not try to fix every finding at once.
  • Repeat after major changes; a review is a snapshot.

§3Pillars Pull Against Each Other

Real designs trade pillars off. Multi-region active-active improves reliability and costs far more. Aggressive cost cutting can hurt reliability and performance. The framework's value is making the trade-off explicit and owned, not eliminating it.

Decision: single region, multi-AZ, daily cross-region backups
Gains:    cost, simplicity
Accepts:  regional outage RTO of ~4h
Owner:    payments team; reviewed quarterly
A review produces decisions The output is not a score. It is a short list of risks with owners, dates, and an explicit statement of what you chose not to fix.

§4Common Mistakes

MistakeWhat HappensFix
Treating the framework as a one-off auditFindings go staleRe-review after significant changes
Fixing the easy items firstHigh-risk issues remainRank by impact and likelihood
Reviewing without the operatorsAnswers don't reflect realityInvolve the team that runs the workload
Practice
  1. Run a Well-Architected review on one small workload and list the top three risks.
  2. Write down one pillar trade-off you accepted and who owns it.
Key takeaways
  • The six pillars form a review structure for any workload.
  • Reviews rank risks, assign owners, and record accepted trade-offs.
  • Pillars conflict; the goal is a conscious, documented balance.
