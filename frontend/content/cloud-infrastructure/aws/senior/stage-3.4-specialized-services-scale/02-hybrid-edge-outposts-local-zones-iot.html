Hybrid & Edge: Outposts, Local Zones & IoT

Some workloads cannot run in a normal Region because of latency, data residency, or connectivity, so AWS extends its infrastructure outward.

§1The Options

OptionWhat it isUse for
Local ZonesAWS compute and storage in metro areas, extending a parent RegionSingle-digit-ms latency for media, gaming, real-time apps
WavelengthAWS infrastructure inside telecom 5G networksUltra-low latency for mobile edge apps
OutpostsAWS-managed racks or servers in your data centerLocal processing, data residency, low latency to on-premises systems
CloudFront / edgeCaching at points of presenceContent delivery, not general compute

§2IoT and Disconnected Operation

AWS IoT Core connects devices over MQTT with per-device certificates and policies, routes messages with rules to other AWS services, and keeps device shadows. IoT Greengrass runs code locally on devices so they keep working when disconnected and sync later. Device identity, secure provisioning, and over-the-air updates are the hard parts at scale.

§3Design Considerations

  • Decide what truly needs to be local; keep everything else in the Region.
  • Plan for intermittent connectivity: queue locally, sync later, tolerate delay.
  • Outposts capacity is fixed: you plan it like on-premises, not infinitely elastic.
  • Operations still need patching, monitoring, and a path for updates at remote sites.
  • Check which services are available in a Local Zone or on an Outpost; the list is narrower than a Region.
Local only what must be local Every workload moved to the edge gains latency and residency benefits and loses elasticity and simplicity. Keep the footprint small and justified.

§4Common Mistakes

MistakeWhat HappensFix
Assuming all Region services exist at the edgeDesign can't be deployedCheck supported services per location
Treating Outposts as infinite capacityCapacity exhaustionPlan capacity up front
Ignoring device identity and updates in IoTInsecure, unmanageable fleetProvision per-device certificates and plan OTA updates
Practice
  1. Choose between Local Zones, Wavelength, and Outposts for three scenarios and justify each.
  2. Sketch how a device behaves when connectivity drops for an hour.
Key takeaways
  • Local Zones, Wavelength, and Outposts extend AWS closer to users or data.
  • IoT needs device identity, local processing, and update strategy.
  • Keep the edge footprint minimal and plan for disconnection.
