Three levels, twelve stages — accounts, core services, data and delivery, and good habits first, then scaling, managed data and messaging, security operations, and infrastructure as code, built toward architecture, governance, cost, migration, and the judgment to design systems and defend the trade-offs.

Checkpoints are deliberately unscaffolded. If you can finish one without hints, the stage actually landed, not just the following-along.

Levels aren't strict walls. Identity appears at Junior as users, roles, and least privilege, returns at Intermediate as conditions and boundaries, and comes back at Senior as multi-account governance and federation. Cost follows the same arc: budgets, then lifecycle and rightsizing, then FinOps. The ideas transfer to Azure and Google Cloud even though the product names do not.

Senior is about judgment as much as mechanism. Knowing how to build a multi-AZ application is one skill; choosing a recovery strategy with stated RPO and RTO, and writing the decision so a skeptical reviewer accepts it, is another, and the last stage is built around that gap.
