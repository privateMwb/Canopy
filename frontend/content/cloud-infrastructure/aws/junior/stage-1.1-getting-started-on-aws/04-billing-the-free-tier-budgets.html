Billing, the Free Tier & Budgets

AWS charges for what you use, so understanding the pricing dimensions and setting budget alerts early prevents the classic surprise bill.

§1How AWS Charges

Pricing is usage-based, usually along a few dimensions: compute time, storage per GB-month, requests, and data transfer out. Inbound data is generally free; data leaving AWS to the internet or crossing regions is not.

ServiceTypical charge dimensions
EC2Instance-seconds, EBS storage, data transfer
S3GB stored, requests, data transfer out
NAT GatewayPer hour plus per GB processed
Public IPv4 addressPer hour, including idle ones

§2The Free Tier

New accounts have received a free-tier offering whose structure AWS has revised over time, so read the current terms in the Billing console rather than relying on old blog posts. Some services also have always-free allowances, and others offer short trials. Free tiers cover limited usage; going past them charges normally.

§3Budgets and Alerts

AWS Budgets lets you set a monthly cost threshold and email you when actual or forecasted spend crosses it. Set one before building anything.

aws budgets create-budget --account-id 123456789012 \
  --budget file://budget.json \
  --notifications-with-subscribers file://notify.json

Also look at Cost Explorer to see spend by service, and enable billing alerts or Cost Anomaly Detection to catch unusual jumps.

Budgets alert you; they do not stop spending. Acting on an alert is up to you or your automation.

Alerts are not caps A budget notifies you after spend rises. It will not shut resources off by default, so set a low threshold and check the email address is monitored.

§4Common Mistakes

MistakeWhat HappensFix
Not setting any budgetThe first sign of a problem is the invoiceCreate a low monthly budget with email alerts first
Ignoring data transfer and NAT costsSurprise charges on a quiet appCheck pricing pages for every service you add
Assuming free tier covers everythingCharges start silently past limitsRead the current free-tier terms in the console
Practice
  1. Create a budget at a small amount with an alert at 80% of actual spend.
  2. Open Cost Explorer and group last month's cost by service.
Key takeaways
  • Costs come from usage: compute, storage, requests, and data transfer out.
  • Check the current free-tier terms; do not assume.
  • Set a budget alert before you build; it notifies but does not cap.
