Global Infrastructure: Regions, Availability Zones & Edge

AWS is built from Regions, each made of isolated Availability Zones, plus an edge network closer to users.

§1Regions and Availability Zones

A Region contains multiple Availability Zones (AZs): one or more discrete data centers with independent power, cooling, and networking, connected by fast low-latency links. Most regions have three or more AZs.

aws ec2 describe-availability-zones --region eu-west-1 \
  --query 'AvailabilityZones[].ZoneName'

Spreading across AZs protects against the loss of one data center. Spreading across Regions protects against a region-wide event, but costs more and is harder.

§2Edge Locations

Edge locations are points of presence used by services such as CloudFront (content delivery) and Route 53 (DNS) to serve users from nearby. They cache content and shorten the network path, but your actual workload still runs in a Region.

§3Designing With Geography

ConcernChoose by
Latency to usersNearest region, plus CloudFront for static content
ResilienceMultiple AZs in one region first
ComplianceRegions that satisfy data residency rules
Service availabilityNot every service or feature exists in every region
PricePrices differ between regions

AZ names such as eu-west-1a are mapped differently per account, so your 1a may not be a colleague's 1a. Use AZ IDs (such as euw1-az1) when coordinating across accounts.

AZ is the unit of failure Think of one AZ as the thing that can fail. Putting everything in one AZ means one outage takes you down; two or three AZs means it does not.

§4Common Mistakes

MistakeWhat HappensFix
Running everything in one AZOne data center issue causes full downtimeSpread across at least two AZs
Assuming every service exists in every regionDeployment fails or features are missingCheck regional service availability up front
Treating edge as where code runsExpecting server-side state at the edgeKeep workloads in a Region; use the edge for caching and DNS
Practice
  1. List the AZs in your default region and in one other region.
  2. Pick a region for a hypothetical app with EU customers and justify it in three bullet points.
Key takeaways
  • A Region is a set of isolated AZs; AZs are the main unit of failure to design around.
  • Edge locations speed delivery but do not host your main workload.
  • Region choice balances latency, compliance, service availability, and price.
