Terraform, OpenTofu, Pulumi & CloudFormation

Terraform has company. A licensing change created a fork, and several other tools solve the same problem differently. Choose on facts that matter to your team.

§1The Licensing Story

In August 2023, HashiCorp moved Terraform and its other products from the open-source MPL license to the Business Source License (BSL), which restricts some competitive commercial use. In response, a community group created OpenTofu, a fork of the last open-source Terraform, now under the Linux Foundation and released under MPL 2.0. HashiCorp was later acquired by IBM.

For most teams using Terraform internally, the license does not restrict their use. It matters most to vendors building products on top of Terraform, and to organizations with policies about open-source licensing.

§2OpenTofu

OpenTofu is largely drop-in compatible with Terraform's configuration and providers, and it has its own registry. It has also developed its own features, such as state and plan encryption and provider iteration with for_each, while Terraform has developed others of its own, such as ephemeral resources and Stacks. The two are diverging, so check each tool's current release notes before depending on a feature.

§3Alternatives

ToolApproachNotable tradeoff
PulumiGeneral-purpose languages (TypeScript, Python, Go, and more)Real programming power, with a different model for state and review
AWS CloudFormationAWS-native templates, state managed by AWSDeep AWS integration and rollback, but AWS only
AWS CDK, BicepCode or higher-level syntax that compiles to native templatesCloud-specific, less portable
CrossplaneCloud resources as Kubernetes objectsStrong for platforms built on Kubernetes

CDK for Terraform, which let you write Terraform in general-purpose languages, was deprecated by HashiCorp. Verify its current status before adopting it, and look at Pulumi or plain HCL instead.

§4How to Choose

Weigh the ecosystem (providers and modules), your team's skills, multi-cloud needs, governance and policy tooling, hiring, and the cost of switching. Most teams are best served by one tool and a strong set of practices, not by chasing features across tools.

Your configuration is the real investment Providers, modules, and conventions carry most of the value, and for Terraform and OpenTofu most of them transfer. Judge a switch by the migration cost of your own code and process, not by headlines.

§5Common Mistakes

MistakeWhat HappensFix
Switching tools over a licensing headlineMigration cost with little practical gainCheck whether the license affects your actual use

Assuming full parity foreverFeatures diverge and break portabilityUse the shared subset, and read release notes before upgrading
Practice
  1. Run tofu init on an existing Terraform configuration in a sandbox and note any differences in providers or output.
  2. Write a one-page recommendation for your team, naming the three criteria that would decide between Terraform, OpenTofu, and Pulumi.
Key takeaways
  • Terraform's BSL license led to the OpenTofu fork; the tools are similar but diverging.
  • Pulumi, CloudFormation, CDK, and Crossplane make different tradeoffs.
  • Choose on ecosystem, skills, and migration cost, not on headlines.
