StatefulSets & Headless Services

StatefulSets give pods stable names, stable network identity, and their own storage, which is what databases and clustered systems require.

§1What Deployments Cannot Give

Deployment pods are interchangeable and anonymous: random names, shared template, any pod can replace any other. A database replica, a Kafka broker, or an etcd member needs more: a stable identity so peers can find it, and its own disk that follows it across restarts. A StatefulSet provides both.

§2Properties of a StatefulSet

  • Ordered, stable names: db-0, db-1, db-2. A replaced pod keeps its name.
  • Stable storage: each pod gets its own PVC from volumeClaimTemplates, reattached when the pod is recreated.
  • Ordered operations: pods start in order (0, 1, 2) and stop in reverse, by default. podManagementPolicy: Parallel relaxes this.
  • Stable DNS through a headless Service.

§3Headless Services

A normal Service has one virtual IP. A headless Service (clusterIP: None) has none; DNS returns the pod addresses directly, and each pod gets its own record.

apiVersion: v1
kind: Service
metadata:
  name: db
spec:
  clusterIP: None
  selector:
    app: db
  ports:
    - port: 5432
---
apiVersion: apps/v1
kind: StatefulSet
metadata:
  name: db
spec:
  serviceName: db               # ties pods to the headless Service
  replicas: 3
  selector:
    matchLabels:
      app: db
  template:
    metadata:
      labels:
        app: db
    spec:
      containers:
        - name: postgres
          image: postgres:16
          volumeMounts:
            - name: data
              mountPath: /var/lib/postgresql/data
  volumeClaimTemplates:
    - metadata:
        name: data
      spec:
        accessModes: ["ReadWriteOnce"]
        resources:
          requests:
            storage: 20Gi

Each pod is reachable at db-0.db.<namespace>.svc.cluster.local, so a replica can always find the primary by name.

§4Updates and Storage Lifecycle

StatefulSets update pods one at a time in reverse order. updateStrategy.rollingUpdate.partition lets you roll out to only pods with an ordinal at or above a number, which is a built-in canary.

Deleting a StatefulSet does not delete its PVCs, deliberately protecting data. Newer releases offer persistentVolumeClaimRetentionPolicy to choose automatic cleanup on scale-down or delete.

A StatefulSet is not a database It guarantees identity and storage. Replication, failover, backups, and upgrades are still your job, which is why operators (a Senior-level topic) exist for databases, and why many teams use managed databases.

§5Common Mistakes

MistakeWhat HappensFix
Using a Deployment for a clustered databasePods lose identity and share one diskUse a StatefulSet with volumeClaimTemplates
Forgetting the headless ServicePods have no stable DNS namesCreate it and set serviceName
Assuming deleting the StatefulSet removes dataOrphaned PVCs keep costing moneyClean up PVCs deliberately, or set a retention policy
Practice
  1. Deploy a three-replica StatefulSet and verify that deleting db-1 brings back a pod with the same name and volume.
  2. Resolve db-0.db from another pod using nslookup.
Key takeaways
  • StatefulSets provide stable names, per-pod volumes, and ordered rollout.
  • A headless Service gives each pod its own DNS record.
  • Identity and storage are guaranteed; replication and failover are not.
