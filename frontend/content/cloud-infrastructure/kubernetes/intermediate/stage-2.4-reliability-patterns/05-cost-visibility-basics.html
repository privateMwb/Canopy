Cost Visibility Basics

Kubernetes makes it easy to spend money invisibly, so cost visibility starts with labels, honest requests, and the habit of attributing spend to the teams that cause it.

§1Where the Money Goes

In a cluster, you pay for nodes (and their disks and network), not for pods directly. The cost of a workload is its share of the nodes it occupies. Several things inflate the bill:

  • Over-requested resources: requests reserve capacity whether used or not.
  • Idle and underused nodes: poor bin packing or fragmentation leaves capacity stranded.
  • Orphaned resources: forgotten PVCs, load balancers, snapshots, and unattached disks.
  • Network egress and cross-zone traffic, which is often a surprise.
  • Non-production environments running around the clock.

§2Labels as the Foundation

Attribution requires consistent metadata on every workload and namespace:

metadata:
  labels:
    team: payments
    cost-center: cc-1042
    app.kubernetes.io/name: checkout
    env: prod

Enforce this with admission policy or CI checks so that unlabeled workloads do not reach the cluster. Namespace-per-team makes attribution simpler still.

§3Allocating Cost

A common method divides a node's cost among its pods in proportion to their requests (or to the greater of request and actual usage). Tools do this for you:

ToolNotes
OpenCostOpen source CNCF project for Kubernetes cost allocation
KubecostBuilt on OpenCost, with additional features and a commercial tier
Cloud provider cost toolsGKE cost allocation, AWS split cost allocation data, Azure cost analysis
Prometheus queriesRoll your own from kube_pod_container_resource_requests
# CPU requests by team (requires kube-state-metrics and labels on namespaces or pods)
sum by (label_team) (
  kube_pod_container_resource_requests{resource="cpu"}
  * on(namespace, pod) group_left(label_team) kube_pod_labels
)

§4Finding Savings

  1. Compare requests with usage and reduce the biggest gaps first (the right-sizing practice from earlier).
  2. Scale non-production down outside working hours.
  3. Delete orphans: unbound PVs, unused load balancers, stale snapshots.
  4. Use cheaper capacity for tolerant workloads, such as spot or preemptible nodes for stateless batch work, with the right tolerations and disruption handling.
  5. Reduce cross-zone chatter where latency and resilience allow.
Show teams their own numbers Cost tends to drop when owners see a weekly report of what their workloads cost, rather than when a central team guesses what to cut.

§5Common Mistakes

MistakeWhat HappensFix
No ownership labelsCosts cannot be assigned to anyoneRequire labels and namespaces per team
Only watching node countWaste inside requests goes unseenTrack request-versus-usage efficiency
Leaving orphaned disks and load balancersSilent recurring chargesAudit unattached resources on a schedule
Practice
  1. Label two namespaces with owners and write a query showing CPU requests per team.
  2. List five places in your own cluster where spend could hide, and how you would check each.
Key takeaways
  • You pay for nodes, so cost follows requests, packing efficiency, and forgotten resources.
  • Consistent labels and namespaces make cost attributable.
  • Show owners their spend, and start with the largest request-to-usage gaps.
