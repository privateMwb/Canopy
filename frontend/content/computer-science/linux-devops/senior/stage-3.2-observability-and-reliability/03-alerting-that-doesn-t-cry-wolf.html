Alerting That Doesn't Cry Wolf

A good alert means a human must act now; everything else is a dashboard, a ticket, or noise that trains people to ignore the pager.

§1Alert on Symptoms

Page on what users experience, such as high error rate or high latency, not on every possible cause such as CPU at 85 percent. Causes are for dashboards and diagnosis, and symptoms are for paging. Every page should be urgent, actionable, and require a human. If it is not, it should be a ticket or should not exist.

# good page: users are hurting
checkout error ratio > 2% for 5 minutes
# bad page: a cause that may not matter
node CPU > 80%
# ticket, not page: needs attention this week
disk will fill in 6 days (predict_linear)

§2Burn-Rate Alerts

A burn rate is how fast you are consuming the error budget. A rate of 1 spends it exactly over the SLO window, and a rate of 14.4 sustained for an hour burns 2 percent of a 30-day budget. Alerting on burn rates over two windows catches fast outages quickly and slow leaks reliably, while ignoring blips.

# page: fast burn (budget gone in about 2 days)
burn_rate(1h)  > 14.4  AND  burn_rate(5m)  > 14.4
# page: slower, sustained burn
burn_rate(6h)  > 6     AND  burn_rate(30m) > 6
# ticket: slow leak
burn_rate(3d)  > 1

The short window confirms the problem is still happening, so the alert resolves quickly after a fix instead of lingering.

§3Make Every Alert Usable

An alert should say what is broken, how bad it is, and where to start. Link a runbook and a dashboard. Review alerts regularly: delete the ones nobody acts on, and for each page ask "what did the responder do?"

alert: CheckoutHighErrorRate
severity: page
summary: "Checkout error ratio {{ $value | humanizePercentage }} (SLO burn rate high)"
runbook: https://wiki.acme.dev/runbooks/checkout-errors
dashboard: https://grafana.acme.dev/d/checkout
# track weekly: pages per on-call shift, % actionable, time to acknowledge
Alert fatigue is a reliability problem Noisy pagers make people mute or ignore alerts, and then the real one gets missed. Protecting the on-call engineer's attention is part of protecting the system.

§4Common Mistakes

MistakeWhat HappensFix
Paging on causes like CPU or memoryPages fire when users are fine and nobody can actPage on user-visible symptoms; graph the causes
Alerts with no runbook or contextThe responder starts from zero at 3 a.m.Link a runbook, a dashboard, and an owner
Never pruning alertsNoise accumulates until the pager is ignoredReview pages monthly and delete or demote the unactionable ones
Practice
  1. Audit ten alerts your team has: for each, decide whether it should be a page, a ticket, or deleted.
  2. Write a two-window burn-rate alert for an availability SLO.
Key takeaways
  • Page on user-visible symptoms; use causes for dashboards and diagnosis.
  • Burn-rate alerts across two windows catch both fast and slow budget loss.
  • Every alert needs a runbook, and unactionable alerts should be removed.
