Huge Pages & TLB Reach

Using bigger pages shrinks the number of translations a program needs, which can cut TLB misses dramatically but comes with its own costs.

§1Why Bigger Pages Help

In Stage 2.3 you saw that TLB reach is entries multiplied by page size. With 4 KiB pages, a 1,536-entry TLB covers 6 MiB. With 2 MiB huge pages, the same TLB covers 3 GiB. A program with a large working set, such as a database or an in-memory cache, may take a TLB miss on a large fraction of accesses with small pages and few with huge ones.

Page sizeEntries to map 1 GiBAlso cuts
4 KiB262,144—
2 MiB512One page-table level in the walk
1 GiB1Two page-table levels in the walk

Larger pages also shorten the page walk, since the final levels of the tree are skipped.

§2Two Ways to Get Them

  • Explicit huge pages (hugetlbfs): reserved ahead of time by the administrator, never swapped, and requested by the application with mmap(MAP_HUGETLB)
  • Transparent Huge Pages (THP): the kernel automatically backs suitable aligned regions with 2 MiB pages, with no application changes
cat /sys/kernel/mm/transparent_hugepage/enabled   # [always] madvise never
grep -i huge /proc/meminfo                        # AnonHugePages, HugePages_Total

// Ask for huge pages only on this region (when THP is set to madvise)
madvise(region, size, MADV_HUGEPAGE);

The madvise setting is a common production choice: huge pages only where an application has asked for them.

§3The Costs

  • Internal fragmentation and bloat: a sparsely used 2 MiB region still consumes 2 MiB of RAM
  • Allocation latency: finding 2 MiB of contiguous physical memory may require compaction, causing stalls, especially when memory is fragmented
  • Copy-on-write granularity: after a fork, a write can copy 2 MiB rather than 4 KiB
  • Swapping and splitting: huge pages may be split into small pages under memory pressure

Several database vendors recommend disabling THP because of latency spikes and use explicit huge pages instead. Always measure with your workload.

The key insight Huge pages exchange memory efficiency for translation efficiency. They pay off for large, densely used working sets and can hurt small or sparse ones.

§4Common Mistakes

MistakeWhat HappensFix
Enabling THP system-wide and ignoring latencyCompaction stalls cause unpredictable pausesUse madvise mode, and monitor latency percentiles
Expecting huge pages for small allocationsRegions must be large and alignedReserve huge pages for big, long-lived regions
Not reserving hugetlbfs pages early enoughAllocation fails once memory is fragmentedReserve at boot or early in system startup
Practice
  1. Run a random-access benchmark over a 4 GiB array with THP set to never and always. Use perf stat to compare dTLB misses.
  2. Compute the TLB reach of a 1,024-entry TLB for 4 KiB, 2 MiB, and 1 GiB pages.
Key takeaways
  • Bigger pages multiply TLB reach and shorten page walks.
  • Use hugetlbfs for reserved pages or THP, preferably in madvise mode.
  • The tradeoffs are bloat, compaction latency, and larger copy-on-write units.
