Confidentiality, Integrity & Availability

Almost every security failure breaks confidentiality, integrity, or availability, and naming which one is at stake tells you what kind of defense to reach for.

§1The Three Properties

PropertyQuestion it answersTypical control
ConfidentialityCan only the right people read it?Encryption, access control
IntegrityCan anyone change it without being noticed?Hashes, signatures, input validation, permissions
AvailabilityIs it there when needed?Redundancy, rate limiting, backups

These are often called the CIA triad. Controls for one can weaken another: encrypting backups protects confidentiality, but losing the key destroys availability.

§2Which Attacks Break Which

  • A database dump on a hacker forum breaks confidentiality.
  • A forged request that changes an account's email breaks integrity, because data was modified by someone who shouldn't.
  • A flood of traffic or a deliberately expensive query breaks availability.
  • Ransomware attacks availability, and often threatens confidentiality too by stealing data first.

Many incidents hit several at once, but asking "which property failed first?" usually points to the root cause.

§3Authentication, Authorization, and Accountability

Three companion ideas make the triad enforceable. Authentication proves who someone is, authorization decides what they may do, and accountability (audit logs) records what they did so misuse can be found afterward.

request ──▶ who are you?    (authentication)
        ──▶ may you do this? (authorization)
        ──▶ record that it happened (audit log)
A common confusion Encryption gives confidentiality, not integrity. Without a check that the data was not changed, an attacker can flip bits in ciphertext and the receiver will decrypt garbage or, worse, attacker-chosen data. Stage 2.1 returns to this.

§4Common Mistakes

MistakeWhat HappensFix
Thinking encryption alone makes data safeTampering goes undetected, and keys can still leakPair confidentiality with integrity checks and key protection
Ignoring availability as a security concernA cheap denial-of-service takes the product downPlan limits, quotas, and capacity for abuse
Having no audit trailYou can't tell what an attacker didLog security-relevant actions with who, what, and when
Practice
  1. Classify each as a confidentiality, integrity, or availability failure: a leaked API key, a price edited in a request, a site taken down by traffic.
  2. Name one control that protects confidentiality but could hurt availability.
Key takeaways
  • Confidentiality, integrity, and availability are the three properties to protect.
  • Name which property an attack breaks to choose the right defense.
  • Authentication, authorization, and audit logs make the triad enforceable.
