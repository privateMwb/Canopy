Data Protection & Privacy Engineering

The most reliable way to limit a data breach is to hold less data, keep it for less time, and make what remains less useful to a thief.

§1Minimize and Classify

Data you never collect can't leak. For each field, ask whether you need it, for what purpose, and how long. Then classify what remains, so protections follow sensitivity.

ClassExamplesTypical handling
PublicMarketing pagesNo special controls
InternalRoadmaps, metricsAccess control
Confidential or personalEmails, addresses, order historyAccess control, encryption, logging, retention limits
Highly sensitiveGovernment IDs, health data, payment dataStrict need-to-know, field-level encryption or tokenization, audit

§2Retention and Deletion

Define how long each data type is kept and actually delete it: in the database, backups (on their schedule), search indexes, analytics copies, and logs. Many privacy regimes, such as GDPR, give people rights over their data and require limits on retention, so know what you hold and where, with a data inventory and a deletion process that reaches every copy.

§3Tokenization, Masking, and Pseudonymization

  • Tokenization: replace a sensitive value (a card number) with a random token, keeping the real value in a small, heavily protected vault. Most systems then handle only tokens.
  • Masking: show or log only part of a value (for example, only the last four digits, XXXX-4242), and mask data in non-production copies so developers don't work with real personal data.
  • Pseudonymization: replace identifiers with stable aliases so analysis works without direct identity, remembering that re-identification may still be possible by joining datasets.
  • Field-level encryption for the most sensitive columns, with keys managed as in Stage 2.2.

§4Design for the Breach

Assume a breach will occur and ask what an attacker walks away with. Separate sensitive data into its own store with tight access, avoid copying it into logs, analytics, and caches, and limit what any single service can read. This turns a catastrophic breach into a contained one.

Regulation, briefly Laws such as GDPR, HIPAA, and PCI DSS add specific obligations around consent, retention, and breach notification. Treat them as constraints to understand with legal and compliance colleagues, not as a substitute for the engineering controls above.

§5Common Mistakes

MistakeWhat HappensFix
Collecting data "in case it's useful"More to protect, more to leak, more regulatory exposureCollect only what a defined purpose requires
Deleting from the main database but not from backups, logs, and analyticsData persists in copiesMaintain a data inventory and delete across every copy
Using production data in test environmentsDevelopers and test systems hold real personal dataMask or generate synthetic data
Practice
  1. Build a data inventory for a small app: each field, its purpose, its classification, and where copies live.
  2. Describe how tokenizing card numbers changes what a database breach exposes.
Key takeaways
  • Minimize what you collect and how long you keep it.
  • Classify data and apply controls by sensitivity, including tokenization and masking.
  • Design so a breach exposes as little as possible.
