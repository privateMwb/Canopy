Latency, Bandwidth & Throughput

A fast link can still feel slow, because time to deliver a request and rate of delivering data are two different limits.

§1Three Different Numbers

TermMeaningUnit
LatencyTime for a bit to get from sender to receiver (often quoted as round-trip time, RTT)ms
BandwidthMaximum rate the link can carryMbps, Gbps
ThroughputRate you actually achieveMbps

Throughput is at most the bandwidth, and often far lower because of latency, packet loss, and protocol behavior. Think of a pipe: bandwidth is how wide it is, and latency is how long it is.

§2Where Delay Comes From

  • Propagation: the signal's travel time, limited by physics. Light in fiber covers roughly 200,000 km per second, about 5 microseconds per kilometer.
  • Transmission: time to push all the bits onto the wire: packet size divided by bandwidth.
  • Queuing: waiting in router buffers when traffic is heavy.
  • Processing: time routers and hosts spend inspecting and forwarding.
# Propagation: New York to London, ~5,570 km
#   5,570 km * 5 µs/km = ~28 ms one way, so at least ~56 ms RTT, whatever the bandwidth.

# Transmission: one 1,500-byte packet on a 100 Mbps link
#   12,000 bits / 100,000,000 bps = 0.12 ms

No amount of extra bandwidth can beat the speed of light. Reducing latency means getting closer to users or sending fewer round trips.

§3Why Round Trips Dominate Small Transfers

Loading a small page may need a DNS lookup, a TCP handshake, a TLS handshake, then the request. Each step waits a full round trip, so on a 100 ms RTT path, that's several hundred milliseconds before the first byte of content, even on a gigabit link.

For large transfers the opposite holds. Data in flight is limited by the bandwidth-delay product: bandwidth × RTT. To keep a 100 Mbps path with a 50 ms RTT full, you need 12.5 MB/s × 0.05 s = 625 KB unacknowledged in flight, a theme that returns with TCP windows in Stage 2.1.

ping -c 5 example.com              # RTT: min/avg/max and packet loss
traceroute example.com             # each hop's RTT: where does latency jump?
mtr -rwc 20 example.com            # traceroute + ping combined, with loss per hop
The key insight Small requests are latency-bound and large transfers are bandwidth-bound. Buying more bandwidth won't fix a page that is slow because of round trips.

§4Common Mistakes

MistakeWhat HappensFix
Upgrading bandwidth to fix slow page loadsLatency, not capacity, was the bottleneckReduce round trips: reuse connections, cache, use a CDN
Reading one hop's high traceroute time as the causeRouters deprioritize replies to probes, so the number is misleadingLook at where latency *stays* high on later hops
Using average RTT onlyTail latency and jitter drive user experienceLook at min, max, and loss, and sample over time
Practice
  1. Ping a server on another continent and compare the RTT to the physical lower bound from distance and the speed of light in fiber.
  2. Compute the bandwidth-delay product for a 1 Gbps link with a 20 ms RTT.
Key takeaways
  • Latency, bandwidth, and throughput are three different measures; throughput is what you actually get.
  • Propagation delay is set by physics, so only fewer round trips or closer servers reduce it.
  • Use ping, traceroute, and mtr to measure delay and loss along a path.
