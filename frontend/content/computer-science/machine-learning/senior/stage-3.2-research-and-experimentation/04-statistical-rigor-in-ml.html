Statistical Rigor in ML

Significance, variance across runs, and avoiding the temptation to p-hack your own experiments.

§1Single Runs Lie

Training involves randomness — weight initialization, data shuffling, dropout. The same code, run twice, can produce meaningfully different results. A single comparison ("my model got 91.2%, the baseline got 90.8%") doesn't tell you if that's a real improvement or just noise.

§2Running Multiple Seeds

# run the same experiment several times with different random seeds
results = []
for seed in [1, 2, 3, 4, 5]:
    set_seed(seed)
    acc = train_and_evaluate()
    results.append(acc)

mean(results), std(results)

Reporting a mean and standard deviation across multiple seeds (the same idea as cross-validation's spread, applied to training runs rather than data splits) tells you whether an improvement is consistent or just one lucky run.

§3Is the Difference Actually Significant?

If model A gets 91.2% ± 0.3% and model B gets 90.8% ± 0.4% across several runs, those ranges overlap substantially — the "improvement" may not be distinguishable from noise. A statistical test (like a t-test comparing the two sets of runs) gives a more rigorous answer than eyeballing the means.

§4The Temptation to p-Hack

A specific trap Running an experiment many times and only reporting the best seed, or trying many small variations until one "beats" the baseline by chance, produces results that look significant but aren't reproducible. This is p-hacking, even when done without bad intent — report results honestly across multiple runs, not just the best one.

§5Common Mistakes

MistakeWhat HappensFix
Reporting a single run's result as "the" resultNo way to know if it's a real effect or noiseRun multiple seeds, report mean and spread
Cherry-picking the best of several runsInflated, non-reproducible claims (p-hacking)Report all runs honestly, not just the favorable one
Practice
  1. Given two models' results across 5 seeds each, describe how you'd decide whether the difference is likely real or just noise.
  2. In one or two sentences, explain why reporting only the best of several runs is misleading.
Key takeaways
  • A single training run's result can't distinguish a real improvement from random noise.
  • Running multiple seeds and reporting mean plus spread is the standard way to check consistency.
  • Cherry-picking the best of several runs is a form of p-hacking — always report honestly across runs.
