RLHF & Preference Tuning

How modern language models get aligned to human feedback, after initial pretraining.

§1Why Pretraining Alone Isn't Enough

A language model pretrained purely to predict the next token learns to produce plausible-sounding text — but "plausible" isn't the same as "helpful," "honest," or "what a person actually wants." RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback) is a further training stage that steers the model toward responses people actually prefer.

§2Step One: Collecting Human Preferences

Humans are shown multiple model outputs for the same prompt and asked which they prefer — not asked to write the ideal answer themselves, just to compare and rank. This is far cheaper and more scalable than collecting detailed "correct" answers for every possible prompt.

§3Step Two: Training a Reward Model

# conceptually: a model trained to predict which of two responses humans would prefer
reward_model(prompt, response_A) > reward_model(prompt, response_B)
# trained so this holds whenever humans preferred response A

Those preference comparisons train a separate reward model — its job is to predict how much a human would like a given response, acting as a learned stand-in for human judgment that can then be used at scale.

§4Step Three: Reinforcement Learning Against the Reward Model

The language model is then fine-tuned using reinforcement learning (the policy/reward loop from the previous lesson), treating the reward model's score as the reward signal. The model learns to generate responses that score well according to the reward model — which, if the reward model is good, means responses humans actually prefer.

§5Why This Matters

Pretraining vs. alignment Pretraining teaches a model language and knowledge, broadly. RLHF (and related preference-tuning methods) teaches it how to behave given that knowledge — which responses are actually helpful, which are safe, which match what people asking the question actually wanted.

§6Common Mistakes

MistakeWhat HappensFix
Assuming RLHF teaches new facts or capabilitiesMisunderstanding its actual roleRLHF shapes behavior/style on top of existing pretrained knowledge, it doesn't add new knowledge
Treating the reward model as a perfect proxy for human judgmentThe policy can learn to exploit reward model weaknesses ("reward hacking")Reward models are imperfect approximations — this is an active area of ongoing research
Practice
  1. In your own words, explain why comparing two responses is easier to collect at scale than writing ideal answers.
  2. Describe, in one or two sentences, what role the reward model plays between human preferences and the final fine-tuned model.
Key takeaways
  • RLHF is a post-pretraining stage that aligns a model's behavior with human preferences.
  • Human comparisons train a reward model, which then acts as the reward signal for reinforcement learning.
  • This shapes how a model behaves on top of its pretrained knowledge — it doesn't teach new facts.
