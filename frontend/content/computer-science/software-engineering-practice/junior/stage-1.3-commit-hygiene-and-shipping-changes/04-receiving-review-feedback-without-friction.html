Receiving Review: Feedback Without Friction

Review comments are information about the code, not a judgment about you. Getting good at receiving feedback makes you improve faster and makes teams easier to work with.

§1Read Comments as Information

It is natural to feel stung by criticism of something you worked hard on. Try to separate the code from yourself. A comment says that a reader, who has less context than you, stumbled somewhere. That is useful data even when the suggested fix is wrong.

If you're confused by a comment, ask. "Could you say more about the concern?" is better than guessing or arguing from a misreading.

§2Respond Clearly

Every comment deserves a visible response: make the change, or explain why not. Don't leave threads hanging, and tell the reviewer when you've pushed a fix.

Reviewer:  This loop re-queries the database on every iteration.
You:       Good catch. Moved the query out of the loop (3a9f2c1).

Reviewer:  Consider using a dataclass here.
You:       I'd rather keep a dict, since this shape comes straight from
           the API and changes often. Happy to revisit if it stabilizes.

Fixing a comment by pushing a new commit, rather than rewriting the branch, makes it easy for the reviewer to see what changed.

§3Knowing When to Push Back

Not every suggestion is right, and you are allowed to disagree. Do it with reasons, not feelings, and be willing to be convinced. If a debate drags on, take it to a call or chat; long comment threads rarely converge.

  • Separate must fix (bugs, risks) from preferences (style, taste)
  • Explain your reasoning once, then defer to the team's conventions or the maintainer
  • If you disagree but it's a style matter, consider just making the change and moving on
A useful mindset Every reviewer is doing you a favor by spending their time. Say thanks, respond to everything, and treat the review as a way to ship better code.

§4Common Mistakes

MistakeWhat HappensFix
Taking comments personallyDefensive replies and strained relationshipsRemember the review is about the code and responding to the idea
Silently ignoring a commentThe reviewer can't tell if you saw it or disagreeReply to every comment, even with just 'Done'
Arguing every point at lengthThe PR stalls and goodwill drainsPick the points that matter, explain once, and move on the rest
Practice
  1. Pick an old review thread and rewrite your reply to be clear and specific.
  2. Next time you get a comment you disagree with, write down your reasons before replying.
  3. Respond to every comment on your current pull request, even if only 'Done.'
Key takeaways
  • Treat comments as information about the code.
  • Respond to everything and show what you changed.
  • Push back with reasons, and know which points matter.
