Checkpoints are deliberately unscaffolded. If you can finish one without hints, the stage actually landed, not just the following-along.

One toy language, start to finish. Every lesson builds on Twig, a small language introduced in stage 1.1. Nothing here teaches a production language; the point is the machinery, and snippets use plain pseudocode so you can build Twig in whatever language you like.

Levels aren't strict walls. The AST from Junior becomes a typed tree, then an IR, then machine code. Closures appear as environments, return as upvalues in a VM, and come back at Senior as calling conventions and ABIs. Revisiting a topic at a harder level is expected.

Senior is about judgment as much as mechanism. Knowing how register allocation or a copying collector works and being able to choose, defend, and test a design decision are different skills, and the last stage is built around that gap.
