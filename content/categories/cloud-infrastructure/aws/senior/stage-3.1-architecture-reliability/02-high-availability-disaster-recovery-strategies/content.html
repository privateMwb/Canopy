High Availability & Disaster Recovery Strategies

RPO and RTO state how much data and time you can afford to lose, and they determine which recovery strategy is worth its cost.

§1RPO and RTO

TermMeaningExample
RPO (recovery point objective)Maximum acceptable data loss, measured in time15 minutes means data older than 15 minutes must survive
RTO (recovery time objective)Maximum acceptable downtime1 hour means service restored within an hour

High availability handles small, frequent failures (an instance, an AZ) with little or no downtime. Disaster recovery handles large, rare events (a region loss, corruption, a destructive deploy).

§2The Strategy Spectrum

StrategyIdeaTypical RTO / RPORelative cost
Backup and restoreRestore from backups in another regionHours / hours$
Pilot lightData replicated, minimal core running, scale up on disasterTens of minutes / minutes$$
Warm standbyScaled-down full copy always runningMinutes / seconds-minutes$$$
Multi-site active-activeFull capacity in several regions serving trafficNear zero / near zero$$$$

Cost and operational complexity rise sharply down the table. Choose the cheapest strategy that meets the stated RPO and RTO.

§3Making It Real

  • Set RPO and RTO with the business, per workload, not globally.
  • Replicate data: S3 replication, Aurora Global Database, DynamoDB global tables, cross-region backup copies.
  • Automate recovery with infrastructure as code and runbooks; manual steps slow RTO.
  • Plan for the failback and for data consistency after failover.
  • Keep dependencies in mind: DNS, identity, and third-party services must also survive.

DR you haven't drilled is a guess Recovery times in a document are estimates. Run a drill, time it, and fix what it exposes; the measured number is the real RTO.

§4Common Mistakes

MistakeWhat HappensFix
Setting RTO/RPO without business inputOver- or under-investmentAgree numbers with stakeholders per workload
Replicating data but not configurationRecovery site can't run the appDeploy the stack from code in the recovery region
Never rehearsing failoverSurprises in a real disasterSchedule recurring drills
Practice
  1. Pick a workload and choose a DR strategy for RTO 1h / RPO 5m; justify the cost.
  2. List the dependencies that would block a regional failover.
Key takeaways
  • RPO is tolerable data loss; RTO is tolerable downtime.
  • Strategies range from backup/restore to active-active with rising cost.
  • Drill recovery and automate it to make RTO real.
