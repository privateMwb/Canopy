Incident Response

In an incident you need a calm, repeatable routine for the three common emergencies: a slow cluster, replication lag, and a full disk.

§1A General Routine

  1. Stabilize and communicate: say who is leading, post status updates, and avoid risky experiments.
  2. Observe before acting: look at the dashboards, currentOp, logs, and rs.status().
  3. Form a hypothesis, make one change, and measure.
  4. Record the timeline, for the review afterward.
db.currentOp({ active: true, secs_running: { $gt: 5 } })       // what is running now
db.killOp(opid)                                                 // stop a runaway operation (carefully)
rs.status()

§2Slow Cluster

Look for the cause in this order.

  • A new or changed query: a missing index shows as COLLSCAN and high docs examined in currentOp and the profiler.
  • Cache pressure: evictions by application threads, rising page reads.
  • Resource saturation: CPU, disk queue, connection count.
  • Lock waits or write conflicts: a hot document or a long transaction.

Typical fixes are an index, killing a runaway query, or stopping a bad deploy. Scaling up buys time but does not fix a scan.

§3Replication Lag

Check which member lags and why.

rs.printSecondaryReplicationInfo()

Common causes are a secondary with weaker hardware or a saturated disk, a heavy bulk write, a long-running operation on the secondary, or network trouble. Fixes are slowing the writer, adding resources, or moving heavy reads off that member. Watch the oplog window, because a secondary beyond it needs a full resync.

§4Disk Pressure

A full disk stops writes and can stop the server.

  • Find what grew: db.stats(), collection sizes, the oplog, logs, and a profile collection.
  • Free space safely: remove or archive old data, expire with TTL, or move large data off.
  • Increase the volume size if you can do it online.
  • Remember that deleting documents does not always shrink files, because the space is reused inside the file, and returning it to the OS needs a resync of a member or compact.
The key insight Under pressure, do the reversible and observable things first. Never delete data files or kill processes you do not understand.

§5Common Mistakes

MistakeWhat HappensFix
Changing several things at onceYou cannot tell what helped, or what brokeOne change at a time, with measurement
Restarting nodes as a first reactionCold caches and a possible election make things worseDiagnose first
No review afterwardThe same incident returnsWrite a blameless review with follow-up actions
Practice
  1. Take a test cluster, create a missing-index query under load, and diagnose it with currentOp and the profiler.
  2. Write a one-page checklist for each of: slow cluster, replication lag, and disk almost full.
Key takeaways
  • Follow a routine: stabilize, observe, change one thing, measure, and record.
  • Slow clusters usually trace to queries, cache pressure, saturation, or contention.
  • Lag and disk issues have standard causes and safe fixes, so write them down before the incident.
