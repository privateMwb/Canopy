Checkpoints are deliberately unscaffolded. If you can finish one without hints, the stage actually landed, not just the following-along.

Levels aren't strict walls. Indexes appear at Junior as a constraint, return at Intermediate as a performance tool, and come back at Senior as internal structures. Transactions follow the same arc: ACID basics, then isolation and locking, then replication and failure. Revisiting a topic at a harder level is expected.

Senior is about judgment as much as mechanism. Knowing how WAL and MVCC work is one skill; deciding when Postgres should hold a workload at all, and writing that down for a skeptical reviewer, is another, and the last stage is built around that gap.
