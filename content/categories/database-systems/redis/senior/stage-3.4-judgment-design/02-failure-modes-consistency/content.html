Failure Modes & Consistency

Redis behaves in specific, predictable ways under partitions, restarts, and failover, and your design should be built on those behaviors, not on hopes.

§1Single Instance Restart

All data is lost without persistence. With RDB, you lose writes since the last snapshot. With AOF everysec, roughly the last second. Loading a large dataset can take minutes, during which the node may not accept commands.

§2Primary Failure With Replication

Asynchronous replication means the promoted replica may lack the last writes. Clients experience errors for seconds. After the old primary returns, its unreplicated writes are discarded.

§3Network Partitions

A primary cut off from replicas and Sentinels may keep accepting writes from clients on its side while the other side promotes a new primary. When the partition heals, the old primary becomes a replica and its writes are lost. Mitigate with min-replicas-to-write and min-replicas-max-lag.

min-replicas-to-write 1
min-replicas-max-lag 10

§4Cluster

Writes to slots without a reachable majority stop. With full coverage required, a single dead shard with no replica can stop everything.

§5Consistency Claims

Redis does not offer linearizable reads across replicas, and failover can break the "I wrote it, so I can read it" expectation. Within one primary, commands are strongly ordered. Across failover, they are not guaranteed to survive.

The key insight Design for the case where the last few seconds of writes are gone, and for the case where two clients briefly talked to two different primaries.

§6Common Mistakes

MistakeWhat HappensFix
Assuming acknowledged writes always survive failoverRecent writes are lostDesign operations to be repeatable or reconstructable
Ignoring partition behaviorDivergent writes silently discardedUse min-replicas-* and think through the old primary's writes
Reading from replicas for decisionsStale data causes wrong decisionsRead from the primary when correctness matters
Practice
  1. Write a failure table for your system: restart, primary crash, partition, and full outage, with the data lost and the user impact in each.
  2. Simulate a partition on a three-node setup and observe what happens to writes on each side.
Key takeaways
  • Know the loss window for restart, crash, and failover.
  • Partitions can create divergent writes that are discarded later.
  • Design operations so a few seconds of lost writes are survivable.
