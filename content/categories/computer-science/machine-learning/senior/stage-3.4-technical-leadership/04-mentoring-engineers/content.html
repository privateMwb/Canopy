Mentoring Engineers

Diagnosing why someone is actually stuck, instead of just handing them the fix.

§1Fixing the Bug vs. Fixing the Understanding

Handing someone the corrected code solves today's problem. Helping them understand why they got stuck solves the next ten problems too. Mentoring well means noticing which kind of help a situation actually calls for.

§2Diagnosing, Not Just Answering

When someone asks "why isn't my model learning," the useful response usually isn't the answer directly — it's a question that helps them find it themselves: "What does your loss curve look like?" "Did you check your learning rate?" "Have you verified gradients are actually flowing?" This mirrors exactly the debugging instinct behind loss-curve diagnosis from earlier lessons, now applied to teaching.

§3Recognizing Common Stuck Points

SymptomLikely underlying gap
Confused about why train/test scores differ wildlyHasn't internalized overfitting vs. underfitting
Surprised a "95% accurate" model is uselessHasn't connected class imbalance to misleading metrics
Can't explain why their test set score looks "too good"Likely has a leakage bug, doesn't recognize the pattern yet

Recognizing these patterns lets you address the real gap, not just the surface symptom someone happened to ask about.

§4Giving Feedback That Lands

A useful habit Separate "this is wrong" from "here's the gap in understanding causing it." The first feels like criticism; the second feels like help. Both can be true about the same mistake, but how you frame it changes whether someone learns from it or just feels bad about it.

§5Common Mistakes

MistakeWhat HappensFix
Always just giving the direct answerThe person doesn't build the diagnostic skill to solve it next timeAsk guiding questions when the goal is learning, not just unblocking
Treating every mistake as needing the same level of explanationOver-explaining wastes time, under-explaining leaves gapsGauge the person's actual gap before deciding how much to explain
Practice
  1. A colleague says their model has 99% training accuracy but performs terribly in production. Write two diagnostic questions you'd ask before giving any answer.
  2. In one or two sentences, describe the difference between fixing someone's bug and fixing their understanding.
Key takeaways
  • Good mentoring often means asking diagnostic questions rather than handing over the direct fix.
  • Recognizing common stuck points (overfitting confusion, imbalance blind spots, leakage) lets you address the real underlying gap.
  • Separating "this is wrong" from "here's the gap causing it" changes whether feedback helps someone learn or just feels like criticism.
