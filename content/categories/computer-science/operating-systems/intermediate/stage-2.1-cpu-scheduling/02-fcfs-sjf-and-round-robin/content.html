FCFS, SJF & Round Robin

Three classic algorithms, worked on the same three jobs, show how scheduling policy changes every metric.

§1The Workload

Three jobs arrive at time 0: A needs 8 units, B needs 4, C needs 2. We'll schedule them by each algorithm and compare.

# FCFS (First Come, First Served): run in arrival order A, B, C
|----- A -----|-- B --|C|
0             8       12 14
# Turnaround: 8, 12, 14  -> avg 11.3
# Response:   0, 8, 12   -> avg 6.7

FCFS is simple and fair in order, but suffers the convoy effect: short jobs wait behind a long one.

§2Shortest Job First

SJF runs the job with the least remaining work. It's provably optimal for average turnaround when all jobs arrive together.

# SJF: run C, then B, then A
|C|-- B --|----- A -----|
0 2       6             14
# Turnaround: 2, 6, 14  -> avg 7.3
# Response:   0, 2, 6   -> avg 2.7

The catch: the OS rarely knows how long a job will run, and a stream of short jobs can starve a long one. The preemptive version is Shortest Remaining Time First (SRTF).

§3Round Robin

Round Robin gives each job a fixed time quantum, then moves it to the back of the queue. With a quantum of 2:

|A|B|C|A|B|A|A|
0 2 4 6 8 10 12 14
# Completion: C at 6, B at 10, A at 14
# Turnaround: 14, 10, 6 -> avg 10.0
# Response:   0, 2, 4    -> avg 2.0
AlgorithmAvg turnaroundAvg responseWeakness
FCFS11.36.7Convoy effect
SJF7.32.7Needs job lengths; starvation
Round Robin (q=2)10.02.0Higher turnaround; switch overhead

Choosing the quantum is a tradeoff. Too small, and switching overhead dominates; too large, and Round Robin degenerates into FCFS.

The key insight Round Robin trades turnaround for response time, and SJF trades fairness for turnaround. Notice that no row in the table wins every column.

§4Common Mistakes

MistakeWhat HappensFix
Forgetting that SJF needs to know job lengthTreating it as implementable as-isReal systems estimate length from past behavior
Setting the quantum extremely smallContext-switch overhead consumes the CPUKeep the quantum much longer than a switch, commonly a few milliseconds
Judging Round Robin by average turnaroundIt looks worse than SJFJudge it by response time and fairness, which is what it targets
Practice
  1. Recompute Round Robin for the same jobs with a quantum of 4. How do turnaround and response change?
  2. Add a job D (needs 1 unit) arriving at time 1. Draw the SRTF schedule.
Key takeaways
  • FCFS is simple but suffers convoy delays.
  • SJF minimizes average turnaround but needs foresight and can starve long jobs.
  • Round Robin improves response time at the cost of turnaround and switching overhead.
