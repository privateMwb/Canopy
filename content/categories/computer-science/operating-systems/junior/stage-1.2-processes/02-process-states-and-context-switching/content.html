Process States & Context Switching

A process cycles between running, ready, and blocked, and the OS swaps which one occupies the CPU by saving and restoring its state.

§1The Process Lifecycle

StateMeaningLeaves this state when
NewBeing createdSetup finishes
ReadyRunnable, waiting for a CPUScheduler picks it
RunningExecuting on a coreTime slice ends, it blocks, or it exits
BlockedWaiting for I/O or an eventThe event occurs
TerminatedFinished, awaiting cleanupParent reaps it

A process that waits on a disk read doesn't burn CPU; it moves to blocked, and another ready process runs in the meantime. That's how one core stays busy while many processes wait on slow devices.

# Observe states on Linux: R = running/ready, S = sleeping, D = uninterruptible I/O, Z = zombie
ps -eo pid,stat,comm | head

§2What a Context Switch Does

Switching from process A to B means saving A's registers into A's PCB, then loading B's saved registers from B's PCB. When B resumes, it continues exactly where it stopped.

// Conceptual pseudocode of a context switch
save_registers(&A->saved_regs);
A->state = READY;
switch_address_space(B->page_table);
load_registers(&B->saved_regs);
B->state = RUNNING;

Switching address spaces also invalidates cached address translations, which is part of why switches are costly.

§3Why Context Switches Aren't Free

A switch costs microseconds. The direct cost is saving and restoring state. The larger indirect cost is that the incoming process finds the CPU caches and TLB full of the previous process's data, so it starts slowly.

  • More processes than cores means more switches
  • Very short time slices mean more switching overhead
  • Voluntary switches (blocking on I/O) and involuntary ones (time slice expired) are counted separately
grep ctxt /proc/self/status
Connection to later lessons Who gets picked from the ready queue is the scheduler's job, covered in Stage 2.1. This lesson is only about the mechanism of swapping once a choice is made.

§4Common Mistakes

Equating ready and runningMisreading scheduler behaviorReady means runnable but waiting for a core; running means actually executing

Practice
  1. Use ps -eo stat to count processes in each state on your machine. Which state dominates, and why?
  2. Draw the state diagram for a process that reads a file, computes on the data, then exits.
Key takeaways
  • Processes move between new, ready, running, blocked, and terminated.
  • A context switch saves one process's CPU state and restores another's.
  • The real cost of switching is partly direct and partly cold caches afterward.
