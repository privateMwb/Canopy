Heaps & Priority Queues

Always-sorted access to the minimum or maximum element, in O(log n) per operation.

§1The Problem Heaps Solve

If you repeatedly need "give me the smallest (or largest) remaining item," sorting the whole collection every time is wasteful. A heap maintains just enough order to answer that question in O(1), with O(log n) to add or remove an item.

§2The Heap Property

A min-heap keeps every parent node smaller than or equal to its children (a max-heap does the reverse) — notably, this is a weaker guarantee than a BST's full ordering, which is exactly why heap operations can be faster for this specific use case.

§3Using Python's Built-In Heap

import heapq

heap = []
heapq.heappush(heap, 5)
heapq.heappush(heap, 2)
heapq.heappush(heap, 8)
heapq.heappop(heap)   # 2 — always removes the smallest

Python's heapq implements a min-heap directly on a list. For a max-heap, a common trick is pushing negated values, then negating again on pop.

§4Priority Queues — Heaps With a Purpose

A priority queue is the abstract concept ("process items in order of priority, not arrival order"); a heap is the typical concrete implementation. This is exactly the structure Dijkstra's algorithm used to always process the closest remaining node next.

§5Why Not Just Keep the List Sorted?

ApproachInsertGet min/max
Sorted listO(n) — must shift to maintain orderO(1)
HeapO(log n)O(1)

A fully sorted list gives O(1) access to the minimum, but each insert costs O(n) to maintain full order. A heap gives up full ordering (you can't efficiently ask "what's the 5th smallest?") in exchange for O(log n) inserts — a direct trade worth understanding, not just memorizing.

§6Common Mistakes

MistakeWhat HappensFix
Expecting a heap to support fast arbitrary-index accessHeaps only efficiently expose the min (or max), not arbitrary ranksUse a different structure (sorted array, balanced tree) if you need arbitrary ordering access
Forgetting the max-heap negation trick in PythonAccidentally implements a min-heap when a max-heap was neededNegate values on push and pop for max-heap behavior with heapq
Practice
  1. Using heapq, implement a max-heap via the negation trick, and pop the 3 largest elements from a list.
  2. Explain, in one or two sentences, why a heap trades away full ordering in exchange for faster inserts.
Key takeaways
  • A heap maintains a weaker ordering than a BST — just enough to always know the min (or max) in O(1).
  • Insert and remove are O(log n); getting the min/max is O(1).
  • Priority queues (processing by priority, not arrival order) are typically implemented as heaps.
