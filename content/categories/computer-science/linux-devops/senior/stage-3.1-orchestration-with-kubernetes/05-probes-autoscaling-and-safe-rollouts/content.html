Probes, Autoscaling & Safe Rollouts

Probes tell Kubernetes whether a pod is alive and ready for traffic, and together with autoscaling and rollout settings they make updates safe.

§1Liveness, Readiness, and Startup Probes

Readiness asks "should this pod receive traffic?" and when it fails the pod is removed from Service endpoints. Liveness asks "is this process stuck?" and when it fails the container is restarted. Startup protects slow-starting apps from being killed before they are up.

containers:
  - name: shop
    image: ghcr.io/acme/shop:1.4.2
    readinessProbe:
      httpGet: { path: /ready, port: 8000 }
      periodSeconds: 5
      failureThreshold: 3
    livenessProbe:
      httpGet: { path: /healthz, port: 8000 }
      periodSeconds: 10
      failureThreshold: 3
    startupProbe:
      httpGet: { path: /healthz, port: 8000 }
      failureThreshold: 30
      periodSeconds: 2

Keep liveness checks shallow. If a liveness probe depends on the database and the database is briefly down, every pod restarts at once and turns a small outage into a large one.

§2Rolling Updates

A Deployment replaces pods gradually. maxSurge is how many extra pods may exist during the update, and maxUnavailable is how many may be down. With a correct readiness probe, a new pod joins traffic only when it is ready, and old pods leave only after replacements are ready, which gives zero downtime.

spec:
  replicas: 4
  strategy:
    type: RollingUpdate
    rollingUpdate:
      maxSurge: 1
      maxUnavailable: 0        # never drop below 4 ready pods
  minReadySeconds: 10
kubectl set image deploy/shop shop=ghcr.io/acme/shop:1.4.3
kubectl rollout status deploy/shop
kubectl rollout history deploy/shop
kubectl rollout undo deploy/shop

§3Autoscaling and Disruption Budgets

A HorizontalPodAutoscaler adds or removes replicas based on metrics such as CPU use. A PodDisruptionBudget stops voluntary disruptions, such as node drains during maintenance, from taking down too many pods at once. It requires CPU requests to be set, because utilization is measured against them.

kubectl autoscale deploy shop --cpu-percent=70 --min=3 --max=12
kubectl get hpa
---
apiVersion: policy/v1
kind: PodDisruptionBudget
metadata:
  name: shop
spec:
  minAvailable: 2
  selector:
    matchLabels: { app: shop }
Readiness is what makes it safe Without a real readiness probe, Kubernetes assumes a pod is ready the moment its process starts, and a rolling update can send traffic to pods that cannot serve it. Readiness is the contract that makes automated releases trustworthy.

§4Common Mistakes

MistakeWhat HappensFix
Making liveness check dependenciesOne dependency blip restarts every podKeep liveness shallow; put dependency checks in readiness
No readiness probeTraffic arrives before the app is readyAdd a readiness probe that reflects real ability to serve
Autoscaling with no CPU requestsThe HPA cannot compute utilizationSet requests, then autoscale on them
Practice
  1. Add readiness and liveness probes to a Deployment, then make the app fail readiness and watch the pod leave get endpoints.
  2. Roll out a broken image with maxUnavailable: 0 and show that the old pods keep serving.
Key takeaways
  • Readiness controls traffic, liveness controls restarts, and startup protects slow boots.
  • maxSurge, maxUnavailable, and readiness together give zero-downtime rollouts.
  • HPAs scale on requests, and PodDisruptionBudgets protect availability during maintenance.
