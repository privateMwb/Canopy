Amdahl's Law

Speeding up part of a program improves the whole only as much as that part matters, which caps the benefit of any single optimization or of adding cores.

§1The Law

If a fraction p of a program's running time can be sped up by a factor s, the overall speedup is:

speedup = 1 / ((1 - p) + p / s)

# 80% of the time is improved 10x:
speedup = 1 / (0.2 + 0.8/10) = 1 / 0.28 = 3.57x

# Even an infinite speedup of that part is capped by the other 20%:
limit = 1 / (1 - p) = 1 / 0.2 = 5x

The part you can't improve, the serial fraction, sets a hard ceiling. This is Amdahl's law, and it explains why one heroic optimization often disappoints.

§2Limits of Parallelism

Applied to multicore, s is the number of cores and p is the fraction of work that parallelizes.

Parallel fraction pMaximum speedup (infinite cores)Speedup on 8 cores
50%2×1.8×
75%4×2.9×
90%10×4.7×
95%20×5.9×
99%100×7.5×

A program that is 90% parallel can never exceed 10× on any number of cores, and on 8 cores it reaches only 4.7×. Real programs add overheads for synchronization and communication, so they do worse.

§3Using It Well

  • Profile first. Amdahl's law tells you which part is worth improving: the biggest fraction of runtime, not the part that is easiest or most interesting
  • After each optimization, re-profile. The bottleneck moves, and the next biggest cost becomes the target
  • Attack the serial part when parallelizing. Cutting a 10% serial section to 5% doubles the achievable ceiling
  • Remember Gustafson's observation: when you add resources, you usually also grow the problem, so the parallel part grows and speedups can be larger than Amdahl's fixed-size view suggests
# Find where the time actually goes before deciding what to speed up
perf record -g ./prog && perf report
# or a quick view of the hottest functions:
perf record ./prog && perf report --stdio | head -30
The key insight Optimize the biggest term first, measure again, and repeat. A 10× speedup of code that is 5% of the runtime is worth less than a 10% gain on code that is 70% of it.

§4Common Mistakes

MistakeWhat HappensFix
Optimizing code that isn't the bottleneckLarge effort, negligible overall gainProfile and target the dominant fraction
Expecting linear scaling from adding coresSerial parts and overheads cap itCompute the Amdahl ceiling before parallelizing
Ignoring the serial fractionIt is the limit that matters most at scaleReduce serial work: I/O, setup, merges, locks
Practice
  1. A job is 70% parallelizable. What is the best possible speedup, and what speedup on 16 cores?
  2. Your profile shows one function takes 40% of the runtime. How much faster must you make it to get an overall 1.25× speedup?
Key takeaways
  • Speedup = 1 / ((1 − p) + p/s), and it is capped by 1/(1 − p).
  • The serial fraction limits how much parallel hardware can help.
  • Profile, fix the largest cost, then profile again.
