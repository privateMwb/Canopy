Credential Attacks: Brute Force, Stuffing & Spraying

Most account takeovers aren't clever, they're guessing at scale, and the defenses that work are rate limits, breach awareness, and not letting one stolen password open many doors.

§1Three Kinds of Guessing

AttackHow it worksWhat it exploits
Brute forceMany passwords against one accountWeak passwords, no lockout
Password sprayingA few common passwords across many accountsLockouts that count per account
Credential stuffingEmail and password pairs leaked elsewhere, tried on your sitePassword reuse

Credential stuffing is the most common today: attackers use automated tools and huge leaked lists, and every success is a real, correct password.

§2Defenses That Work

  • Rate limit by account, by IP address, and by combination, with growing delays. Use more than one signal, because attackers rotate IPs. Networking covers the rate-limiting mechanics.
  • Check new passwords against breach lists (for example with a k-anonymity range lookup service) and reject known-leaked ones.
  • Enable MFA, which makes a correct stolen password insufficient.
  • Detect and challenge suspicious patterns, such as many failures or logins from new regions, with CAPTCHAs or step-up checks.
  • Be careful with hard lockouts: they let an attacker lock real users out deliberately, so prefer throttling to permanent locks.

§3Password Policy That Helps

Modern guidance, such as NIST SP 800-63B, favors length over complexity rules: allow long passphrases, permit paste and password managers, check against known-bad lists, and don't force periodic rotation without evidence of compromise. Composition rules ("one symbol, one number") mostly produce predictable passwords like Password1!.

Tie it together Slow hashing protects against a stolen database, while rate limiting and MFA protect against online guessing. These are different attacks and need different defenses.

§4Common Mistakes

MistakeWhat HappensFix
Rate limiting only by IPBotnets spread requests across many addressesCombine account, IP, and behavior signals
Permanent lockout after N failuresAttackers lock victims out on purposeUse temporary, escalating throttling
Forcing complexity but allowing leaked passwordsUsers pick Summer2024! which is already in listsBlock breached and common passwords, and allow long passphrases
Practice
  1. Design rate limits for a login endpoint that stops brute force and spraying without letting an attacker lock out a specific user.
  2. Explain in two sentences why credential stuffing succeeds even against a site with perfect password hashing.
Key takeaways
  • Brute force, spraying, and credential stuffing are different attacks with different signatures.
  • Combine throttling, breached-password checks, and MFA, and avoid permanent lockouts.
  • Favor long passphrases and breach checks over complexity rules.
