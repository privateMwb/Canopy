curriculum / security

From hashing a password to defending a whole system

Three levels, twelve stages — cryptography, web security, and threat modeling, built toward the judgment to model threats, understand how defenses fail in practice, and explain risk to the people who have to act on it.

Checkpoints are deliberately unscaffolded. If you can finish one without hints, the stage actually landed, not just the following-along.

This track builds on the others instead of repeating them. TLS, certificates, HSTS, cookies, CORS, OAuth, and the OWASP API Top 10 are taught in Networking; side channels and Spectre in Computer Architecture; SQL injection and database encryption in SQL; secrets, supply chain, and pipeline hardening in Linux & DevOps. Security picks each of them up where it left off: the cryptography underneath TLS, the attacks that go past what an API lesson can show, and the judgment about which defenses to buy.

Levels aren't strict walls. Passwords appear at Junior as salts and slow hashes, return at Intermediate as key derivation and passkeys, and come back at Senior as breach recovery and incident response. XSS follows the same arc, from escaping output to Content Security Policy to code review. Revisiting a topic at a harder level is expected.

Senior is about judgment as much as mechanism. Knowing how an attack works and deciding which of forty possible threats deserves this quarter's effort, then explaining that choice to someone who has to approve it, are different skills, and the last two stages are built around that gap.
