Attacker Models: Capabilities, Motives & Kill Chains

Different attackers have different goals, skills, and patience, so the defenses worth buying depend on which ones you are realistically facing.

§1Profiling Attackers

AttackerTypical goalTypical methodsWhat mostly stops them
Opportunistic criminalQuick money at scaleMass scans, known exploits, credential stuffing, phishingPatching, MFA, sane defaults
Malicious userFree service, other users' dataAbusing legitimate features, authorization gapsServer-side authorization, rate limits, abuse monitoring
InsiderTheft, sabotage, grievanceExisting access, privilege abuseLeast privilege, auditing, separation of duties
Targeted attackerSpecific data or long-term accessCustom phishing, supply-chain compromise, chaining flaws, patienceDetection, segmentation, hardened identity, incident response

A defense that's overkill for one attacker is inadequate for another. Write down the attackers in scope, and also the ones you're explicitly *not* defending against.

§2Thinking in Stages

Attackers rarely win in one step. The kill chain and the MITRE ATT&CK framework describe intrusions as stages, and each stage is a chance to detect or stop them.

initial access → execution → persistence → privilege escalation
   → credential access → lateral movement → collection → exfiltration

MITRE ATT&CK catalogs the observed techniques at each stage, so you can ask "which techniques could an attacker use against us at this stage, and would we notice?" Defense in depth means that stopping any one stage breaks the chain.

§3Assume Breach

Designing as though an attacker is *already inside* one component changes your questions: what can they reach from here, what would we see, and how fast can we contain it? It shifts effort from only keeping people out toward segmentation, detection, and recovery, the themes of Stage 3.3 and the zero trust discussion in the Networking track.

Capability is not the same as sophistication Many serious breaches use plain methods: stolen credentials, an unpatched known flaw, a misconfigured storage bucket. Match defenses to what attackers actually do before worrying about rare techniques.

§4Common Mistakes

MistakeWhat HappensFix
Designing only for an elite adversaryBasic gaps like missing MFA stay openCover the common attackers first
Never writing down out-of-scope attackersStakeholders assume protection that doesn't existState the attackers you don't defend against, and why
Focusing only on preventionIntrusions go unnoticed for monthsAdd detection and containment at each stage
Practice
  1. For a payments API, list which of the four attackers is most likely and which defense most reduces their success.
  2. Map a phishing-to-data-theft scenario onto six ATT&CK-style stages and mark where you'd detect it.
Key takeaways
  • Defenses must fit the real attackers' goals, access, and methods.
  • Intrusions unfold in stages, so each stage is a chance to detect or break the chain.
  • Assume breach to plan for containment, not only prevention.
