Not Relying on Color Alone

Color is a great way to reinforce meaning and a terrible way to carry it alone. Roughly one in twelve men has some form of color vision deficiency, and everyone loses color on a grayscale display or in bright light.

§1The Rule

WCAG Success Criterion 1.4.1 (Level A) says color must not be the only visual means of conveying information, indicating an action, prompting a response, or distinguishing an element. Add a second cue that does not depend on hue: text, an icon, a pattern, a shape, or position.

The simplest test is to look at your page in grayscale. Chrome DevTools can emulate several kinds of color vision deficiency, including achromatopsia (no color). If information disappears, you have a problem.

§3Links and Charts

Links inside paragraphs are the classic case. If links differ from surrounding text only by color, that color needs at least 3:1 contrast against the surrounding text, and a second cue (usually an underline) on hover and focus. The simplest and most reliable approach is to keep the underline.

p a {
  text-decoration: underline;
  text-underline-offset: 0.15em;
}

/* Charts: pair color with pattern or direct labels */
.series-a { stroke: #1D4ED8; stroke-dasharray: none; }
.series-b { stroke: #B45309; stroke-dasharray: 6 3; }   /* different dash */

For charts, label the lines or bars directly instead of making the reader match colors to a legend. It is better for everyone, and it makes the chart work in grayscale and in print.

The key insight If you remove color and nothing breaks, you are fine. Color should be reinforcement, never the sole carrier of meaning.

§4Common Mistakes

MistakeWhat HappensFix
Marking errors with a red border onlyUsers with color vision deficiency or low vision may not noticeAdd an icon and a text message that states the problem
Links distinguished only by colorLinks disappear into the text for many readersKeep the underline, or ensure 3:1 against the text plus another cue
Charts that require matching colors to a legendUnreadable in grayscale or with color blindnessUse direct labels, patterns, or distinct markers
Practice
  1. Take a screenshot of a form with errors and convert it to grayscale. Can you still tell which fields have problems?
  2. Redesign a two-series line chart so it works without color, using labels, dashes, or markers.
Key takeaways
  • Never use color as the only way to convey meaning (WCAG 1.4.1).
  • Pair color with text, icons, patterns, or position.
  • Test in grayscale, and emulate color vision deficiencies in DevTools.
