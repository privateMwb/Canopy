Law, Standards & Business Cases

Accessibility is a right first, but it is also a legal duty and a business advantage. Knowing the landscape helps you explain why the work matters to people who control budgets.

§1The Legal Landscape

Laws vary by country, but most point to WCAG as the technical yardstick. This is a simplified map, not legal advice, and rules change, so always check current guidance for your region.

RegionLaw or ruleWho it covers
United StatesADA Title II, with a DOJ rule requiring WCAG 2.1 AAState and local governments. Compliance dates were extended by one year in April 2026, to April 2027 for larger entities and April 2028 for smaller ones
United StatesADA Title IIIPrivate businesses open to the public. No specific web technical rule, but courts and lawsuits commonly use WCAG as the benchmark
United StatesSection 508Federal agencies and their ICT procurement
European UnionEuropean Accessibility Act (in force across member states since 28 June 2025)Many consumer products and services, such as e-commerce, banking, and e-books. Typically refers to the EN 301 549 standard, which incorporates WCAG
European UnionWeb Accessibility DirectivePublic sector websites and apps
UK, Canada, othersEquality Act 2010, AODA and the Accessible Canada Act, and similarOrganizations in those jurisdictions

The consistent pattern is that WCAG 2.1 or 2.2 at Level AA is the working target almost everywhere. Meeting it is the safest way to meet most legal requirements at once.

§2The Business Case

Beyond legal risk, accessibility is often good business. The main arguments are:

  • Market reach. Around one in six people live with a disability, and many more have temporary or situational needs. Together with their families and friends, that is a large customer base.
  • Better products. Clear structure, plain language, and reliable keyboard support improve usability for everyone, and tend to reduce support requests.
  • Search visibility. Semantic headings, descriptive link text, text alternatives, and transcripts overlap heavily with good SEO practice.
  • Lower risk and cost. Building accessibly from the start is far cheaper than retrofitting after a complaint or lawsuit.
  • Reputation and talent. Visible commitment helps with trust, procurement, and hiring.

Use these arguments with the audience in mind. Legal and finance teams respond to risk and market size, while product teams respond to usability and retention.

§3Compliance Is the Floor

Meeting a standard is necessary but not the same as being usable. A page can technically conform and still be frustrating, and a page can be pleasant for disabled users while failing a criterion on paper.

A common pattern in real projects:
1. Compliance:  meet WCAG 2.2 AA so the legal floor is covered
2. Usability:   test with real users, including disabled users
3. Process:     bake checks into design and code review
4. Honesty:     publish an accessibility statement with known gaps

The goal of this course is the third and fourth lines. Compliance gets you a baseline, and a habit of building well gets you the rest.

Not legal advice This lesson summarizes the landscape for developers. Obligations depend on your jurisdiction, your type of organization, and the product. For a real decision, ask a lawyer who specializes in accessibility.

§4Common Mistakes

MistakeWhat HappensFix
Waiting for a complaint before actingExpensive retrofits under time pressure, plus reputational damageBuild accessibility into the process from the start
Treating a conformance claim as proof of usabilityA technically compliant product can still fail real usersAdd testing with disabled users to your checks
Using only the legal argumentTeams comply minimally and treat accessibility as a burdenPair the legal case with the user, product, and business benefits
Practice
  1. Find which accessibility law or standard applies to a product you work on (or a sample company), and write one sentence on the standard it refers to.
  2. Write a three-sentence pitch for investing in accessibility, aimed at a product manager rather than a lawyer.
Key takeaways
  • Most laws point to WCAG, usually 2.1 or 2.2 at Level AA, so AA is the practical target.
  • The business case includes reach, usability, search visibility, lower risk, and reputation.
  • Compliance is the floor. Real accessibility comes from process and testing with users.
