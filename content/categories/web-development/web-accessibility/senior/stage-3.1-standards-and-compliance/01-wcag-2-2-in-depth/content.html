WCAG 2.2 in Depth

Senior accessibility work means reading the standard itself, not summaries of it. This lesson covers what changed in WCAG 2.2, how conformance really works, and how to interpret a criterion when the wording is not obvious.

§1What WCAG 2.2 Added

WCAG 2.2 became a W3C Recommendation in October 2023. It is backward compatible: a page that meets 2.2 also meets 2.1 and 2.0, with one exception. Success Criterion 4.1.1 Parsing was removed as obsolete, because modern browsers handle malformed markup consistently. It added nine new criteria, mostly aimed at people with cognitive and motor disabilities and users of touch devices.

§2How Conformance Really Works

A claim of conformance is more specific than "we follow WCAG". The standard defines five requirements for any conformance claim:

  1. Conformance level: A, AA, or AAA, meaning every criterion at that level and below is met.
  2. Full pages: the whole page conforms, not just the main region. A page cannot exclude parts of itself.
  3. Complete processes: if a page is part of a multi-step process (a checkout, an application), every page in the process must conform.
  4. Only accessibility-supported ways of using technologies: features that rely on support the user's assistive technology does not have do not count.
  5. Non-interference: technologies that do not meet the criteria must not block users from the rest of the page.

This is why a "conformance score" is not a thing in WCAG 2.x. Conformance is binary for each criterion, and the failing of one criterion on a page means the page does not conform at that level.

§3Reading a Criterion Like a Specialist

Each success criterion comes with companion documents: Understanding (intent, benefits, examples) and How to Meet (techniques). Learn the document types, because they answer different questions.

DocumentUse it to
Success Criterion textThe normative requirement. This is what a test is measured against
Understanding documentLearn the intent, edge cases, and key terms
Sufficient techniquesKnown ways that meet the criterion, not the only ways
Advisory techniquesIdeas that go beyond the minimum
FailuresDocumented patterns that definitely fail the criterion

Interpretation questions are normal. Is a disabled button exempt from contrast? (Yes, inactive UI components are.) Is a logo? (Yes, logotypes are exempt.) Does 2.5.8 apply to inline links in a sentence? (Inline targets are an exception.) Read the definitions and exceptions carefully, since the wording is deliberate.

2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum): exceptions worth knowing
Spacing:     undersized target OK if a 24px circle centered on it hits no other target
Equivalent:  another control on the page does the same thing at a sufficient size
Inline:      the target is inside a sentence or block of text
User agent:  size is set by the browser and not modified by the author
Essential:   a particular presentation is legally or functionally required
The key insight The success criterion text is the standard. Techniques are examples, and a criterion can be met by methods nobody has documented. When a question is unclear, go back to the text and the definitions, and write down your interpretation so that others can follow it.

§4Common Mistakes

MistakeWhat HappensFix
Claiming "WCAG 2.2 compliant" without scopeUnclear what was tested, and the claim is easy to challengeState the version, level, pages, and date
Treating techniques as the only way to complyRejects valid solutions, or fails things that meet the criterion in other waysJudge against the criterion, using techniques as examples
Testing one page and claiming a multi-step process conformsViolates the complete-processes requirementTest every step of a process end to end
Practice
  1. Choose three of the new WCAG 2.2 criteria. For each, find one place in a product you know that would pass and one that would fail.
  2. Read the Understanding document for one criterion you have always found vague. Write down its exceptions, and one real example of an edge case.
Key takeaways
  • WCAG 2.2 added nine criteria and removed 4.1.1 Parsing, and it remains backward compatible with 2.1 and 2.0.
  • Conformance has five requirements: level, full pages, complete processes, accessibility-supported technologies, and non-interference.
  • The criterion text is normative. Understanding documents and techniques explain it but do not replace it.
