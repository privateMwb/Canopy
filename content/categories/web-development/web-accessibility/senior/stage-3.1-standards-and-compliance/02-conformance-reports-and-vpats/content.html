Conformance Reports & VPATs

Buyers, auditors, and lawyers do not take "we care about accessibility" as evidence. A conformance report states exactly what was tested, how, and what the results were, including the parts that failed.

§1VPAT and ACR

The Voluntary Product Accessibility Template (VPAT) is a blank form, published by the Information Technology Industry Council (ITI). When it is filled in for a particular product, the result is an Accessibility Conformance Report (ACR). Procurement teams, especially in government and education, often request one before buying.

VPAT comes in editions that line up with different standards: WCAG, Revised Section 508, EN 301 549, and an international edition that covers all three. Pick the edition your buyers need.

Conformance level in an ACRMeaning
SupportsThe product meets the criterion with no known defects, or only minor ones
Partially SupportsSome functionality meets it, and some does not
Does Not SupportThe majority of the product fails it
Not ApplicableThe criterion does not apply to this product
Not EvaluatedNot yet assessed (not allowed for WCAG A and AA)

§2What a Credible Report Contains

A useful report is specific. The remarks column does the real work, because it is where you say what works and what does not.

  • Scope: product name and version, the exact pages or screens, platforms and browsers.
  • Standard: WCAG version and level (for example, 2.2 AA), and any extra criteria.
  • Method: automated tools used, manual testing, and the assistive technologies, with versions.
  • Date and author: when it was done and by whom, including whether it was an internal or independent evaluation.
  • Per criterion: the level of support plus remarks that name the problem, the location, and the workaround if any.
An honest ACR row
Criterion:  1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum) (Level AA)
Level:      Partially Supports
Remarks:    Body text and primary controls meet 4.5:1. Secondary buttons in the
            Reports module (#8A8A8A on #FFFFFF, 3.4:1) do not. Fix scheduled
            for release 4.2 (March).

A weak row
Criterion:  1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum) (Level AA)
Level:      Supports
Remarks:    The product follows WCAG.

§3Evidence and Honesty

A report is only as good as the testing behind it. For websites, the W3C's WCAG-EM (Website Accessibility Conformance Evaluation Methodology) gives a structured way to define scope, choose a representative sample of pages and states, evaluate them, and report. Sampling matters because you cannot test every page, but you must test the template types and the complete user flows.

  • Keep the test evidence: screenshots, tool output, test scripts, and notes. An auditor may ask.
  • Mark partial support as partial. A report that says "Supports" for everything invites scrutiny and legal risk when a customer finds a counterexample.
  • Update the report as the product changes. A two-year-old ACR for a product that ships monthly is misleading.
  • Consider independent evaluation for high-stakes products, since self-assessments are viewed with more caution.

Be careful with the temptation to optimize for the document instead of the product. The purpose of an ACR is to inform buyers, and an accurate report with known gaps and a remediation timeline is worth more than a clean one that will not survive contact with an assistive technology user.

The key insight The remarks column is the report. A table of "Supports" with no explanations is a marketing document, and experienced buyers treat it as one.

§4Common Mistakes

MistakeWhat HappensFix
Marking every row "Supports"Not credible, and a legal liability if challengedReport partial and non-support, with specific remarks and plans
A report with no scope, date, or methodNo one can tell what it covers or whether it is currentState the version, pages, tools, assistive technologies, and date
Letting the report go staleIt describes a product that no longer existsUpdate at each major release, or at least annually
Practice
  1. Find a public ACR from a software vendor. Read it critically: is the scope clear, are the remarks specific, and how many rows are "Partially Supports"?
  2. Write the remarks for three rows for a product you know, including one that fails, with the location and a timeline.
Key takeaways
  • A VPAT is the template. An ACR is the filled-in report for a specific product, edition, and standard.
  • Specific remarks, clear scope, method, and dates are what make a report credible.
  • Use WCAG-EM for sampling and keep evidence. Report partial support honestly, and keep the report current.
