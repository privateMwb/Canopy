Domains, HTTPS & DNS Basics

Pointing a domain at your app.

§1From Name to Your App

A domain is a name that DNS (the internet's phone book) maps to an address. To serve your app at acme.com you buy the name from a registrar, then create DNS records that point it at your host.

RecordMapsTypical use
AName → IPv4 addressApex domain such as acme.com
AAAAName → IPv6 addressIPv6 equivalent of A
CNAMEName → another nameSubdomains such as www or app
TXTName → textOwnership verification, email authentication

§2Connecting a Domain

  1. Add the domain in your hosting dashboard.
  2. The platform tells you exactly which records to create (often an A record for the apex and a CNAME for www).
  3. Add them at your registrar or DNS provider.
  4. Wait for propagation, then confirm the platform shows the domain as valid.
dig acme.com +short            # what does the apex resolve to?
dig www.acme.com CNAME +short  # where does www point?

DNS changes aren't instant because resolvers cache records for the length of their TTL (time to live). Lowering the TTL before a planned change makes switchovers faster.

§3HTTPS Is Not Optional

HTTPS encrypts traffic and proves the server owns the domain, through a certificate. Modern platforms issue and renew certificates automatically (usually from Let's Encrypt) once DNS points correctly. You should also redirect www to the apex (or the reverse) so there is one canonical address.

Pick one canonical hostname Serving the same site at both acme.com and www.acme.com splits SEO signals and cookies. Choose one, and redirect the other to it with a permanent redirect.

§4Common Mistakes

MistakeWhat HappensFix
Using a CNAME on the apex domainMany DNS providers reject it or break email recordsUse an A record (or your provider's ALIAS/ANAME) for the apex
Expecting DNS changes to apply immediatelyConfusion while old values are still cachedAllow time for TTL; verify with dig
Deleting the verification TXT record afterwardsThe platform can lose ownership of the domainLeave the records the platform asks you to keep
Practice
  1. Use dig to look up the A and CNAME records for a site you know and explain what each answer means.
  2. Connect a domain or subdomain to a deployed project and confirm HTTPS works.
Key takeaways
  • DNS maps names to addresses; A records point at IPs and CNAMEs point at other names.
  • Records are cached according to their TTL, so changes take time to appear.
  • HTTPS certificates are automatic on modern platforms, and a single canonical hostname avoids duplication.
