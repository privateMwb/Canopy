Lab Data vs. Field Data

Lighthouse tells you what happened on one machine, and real users tell you what happens in the world. You need both and they often disagree.

§1Two Kinds of Data

Lab data is collected in a controlled, repeatable environment: Lighthouse, WebPageTest, or DevTools throttling a simulated device and network. Field data, also called real-user data or RUM, is collected from actual visitors' browsers.

Lab dataField data
EnvironmentControlled and fixedWildly varied devices and networks
RepeatabilityHighNoisy; varies day to day
Debugging detailRich: traces, waterfalls, screenshotsThin: numbers and attribution
Shows real experienceNoYes
Available before launchYesNo: needs real traffic
Captures user interactionBarelyYes: INP needs real input

§2Why They Disagree

It is common for Lighthouse to say 95 while real users have a poor LCP. This is not a contradiction. Lab tests load a single page, once, with a cold cache, on a fixed simulated device. Real visitors differ in all of these ways.

  • Device and network mix: your users may be slower than the lab profile.
  • Logged-in and personalized views: the lab usually tests the anonymous page.
  • Interactions and scrolling: the lab doesn't click around, so it can't measure INP or later layout shifts.
  • Geography and caching: real users hit CDN edges, warm caches, and slow regions.

Public field data is available without installing anything. The Chrome UX Report (CrUX) aggregates real Chrome users' metrics per origin and page, and tools like PageSpeed Insights show it above the lab results.

§3Using Both Together

Use field data to decide what to work on and lab data to work out how to fix it.

  1. Check field data to see which vital is failing and for which pages and segments.
  2. Reproduce the problem in the lab, matching the device and network of the affected users.
  3. Use the lab traces to find the cause and try a fix.
  4. Ship, then confirm in field data that real users improved. This takes days, because field data is aggregated over a rolling window.
Which do I trust? When they disagree, the field is the truth about users and the lab is a tool for investigation. Treat a good lab score as necessary, never as sufficient.

§4Common Mistakes

MistakeWhat HappensFix
Celebrating a Lighthouse 100 without checking real usersReal visitors may still have a poor experienceCheck CrUX or your own RUM data
Expecting field data to change the day after a fixIt's aggregated over a window, so improvement shows slowlyWait and watch the trend, and use the lab to confirm sooner
Using lab data to judge INPThe lab has no real user inputMeasure INP in the field and use traces to debug
Practice
  1. Run a well-known site through PageSpeed Insights. Compare the field data panel with the lab results and note one difference.
  2. Explain, in two sentences, why a page might score 95 in Lighthouse and still fail LCP in the field.
Key takeaways
  • Lab data is repeatable and detailed, while field data is representative of real users.
  • They disagree because real devices, networks, caches, and interactions differ from the lab.
  • Let field data choose the work, and use lab data to diagnose and test fixes.
